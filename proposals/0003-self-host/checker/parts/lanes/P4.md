- **The statement is §4.12's (README 686-688), verbatim:**

```idris
export
heaviest : (g : Graph) -> (inner : List (Fin (length g.arcs))) -> (0 _ : All IsFinite (weights g inner)) ->
           (start : Vect g.size Nat) ->
           Either (c ** as ** Positive g c as) (pot ** All (Satisfies pot) (map (arc g) inner))
```

  What the statement holds:
  - The precondition makes "no Stuck arc" a type, which G3 obtains from P1's `finite g inner`. Without it, a Stuck arc on no cycle leaves neither certificate inhabited.
  - With `inner`, a component's cycle comes in g's own indices.
  - With `start`, G3's whole-graph recheck (§4.10 step 5) always gets a certificate.
- **Weights:** the two naturals of §4.1. An arc relaxes when `(entry p acc).value + loss < source + gain`, the origin's source being 0. Every inequality is by cross-addition. `minus` is used once, where the relaxed value is computed, after the comparison shows it exact.
- **The algorithm, fixed:**
  1. Look up the listed arcs once (`look`, through P1's `lookUp`, using the precondition), so a round costs one pass, not a list index per arc.
  2. The values after 0 rounds are `start`, each with the empty walk `Start v`.
  3. Then, for at most `length inner` rounds, check (`settle`):
     - every listed arc satisfied: return `Right (values t ** fs)`;
     - else, if rounds are left: one round;
     - else: the arc that still relaxes closes a cycle (`climb`), returned as `Left`.

  The recursion is structural on the rounds left, with the erased `k + left = length inner`.
- **A start that already satisfies every inner arc comes back unchanged.** The first check settles on the values after 0 rounds, which are `start`. The sketch proves this as `0 initialValues : values (initial g inner start) = start` (through `columnTable`); keep the lemma. T3's least-potential check relies on it.
- **Rounds read the round before** (synchronous). `relax` compares with and writes into `acc`, but takes the source's value and walk from `old`. That is what keeps each value's walk within k arcs (`Best.within`), the induction `roundDominates` rests on. A round that read its own writes could add two arcs to one walk in one round.
- **`length inner` rounds.** The pigeonhole runs over the targets of the listed arcs (`universe g inner`, of length `length inner`), not over all positions. So a component's run costs by its own arcs, and a walk of `length inner + 1` arcs repeats a target.
- **No flag and no recheck.** The check after a round is the decision, and its `Settles` is the potential. There is no "changed" flag, no predecessor array (the stored walk is the predecessor chain), no call of P1's `feasible` or `positive`, and no internal error. Each impossible case is `void` of an erased proof.

```idris
||| Lemma 2, constructively: on finite arcs, a cycle whose gains exceed its
||| losses, or a potential every arc satisfies.
|||
||| Bellman-Ford on heaviest walks. A walk starts at a position with the
||| start potential's value there, or at the origin with 0, and follows the
||| listed arcs, its weight kept as what it gains and what it loses. After k
||| rounds each position holds the weight of a walk of at most k arcs that
||| ends there, with the walk, and no walk of at most k arcs ends there
||| heavier: a round reads only the round before, so this holds by
||| induction on the walk.
|||
||| After each round the arcs are checked. If none relaxes, the values are
||| the potential. If one still relaxes after as many rounds as there are
||| arcs, the walk it extends is one arc too long, so two of its arcs enter
||| one position, and between them lies a cycle. Had that cycle gained no
||| more than it lost, cutting it out would leave a walk short enough for
||| the values to dominate and no lighter, which the relaxing arc says it is
||| not. So the cycle rises. The rounds are counted, every check is the
||| machine's comparison, and every proof is erased.
```
