# 5.4 modular: Modular's MLIR C++ and Mojo

This lane owns Modular's engineering: its C++, passes, analyses,
solvers, threading and rules. The design of Mojo's IR as a dialect
design (its ops, types, syntax, effects) is §5.8's.

- A map of `third_party/modular`: where its MLIR C++ lives, its
  dialects, its ODS conventions, how it classifies ops (traits,
  interfaces), its effects, passes and analyses, any solvers of its own
  and how they are threaded, and its C++ style (standard, templates,
  concepts, build flags).
- What we already took from it: the port of
  `Mojo/lib/Transforms/EliminateDuplicateFunctions.cpp` (PINS.md, around
  line 795, may be read for this) and its `CallGraphUtils.h`; what else
  is worth taking, under its licence (Apache-2.0 WITH LLVM-exception).
- Mojo as a model of compile-time parameters, traits and
  monomorphization (`sources/docs/mojo`, its `docs/`, its `stdlib/`):
  what it teaches a monomorphized C++ design and our specialization.
- Its `AGENTS.md`, `CLAUDE.md` and contribution rules for code written by
  agents: what they enforce and how.

#### 5.4.1 Findings

**F1. The tree is the Mojo compiler, not a thin wrapper** (*measured*,
`find ... | wc -l`): `Mojo/` holds about 281,000 lines of C++ and
TableGen (`Mojo/lib`, `Mojo/include`), `Support/` about 55,000, `AsyncRT/`
about 15,000. Dialects with ODS: KGEN, POP, LIT, CO, HLCF, DepGraph,
Interpreter (`Mojo/include/Mojo/*Dialect`), and in `Support/` the M and
DebugInfo dialects. Passes: `Mojo/lib/Transforms` (41 files, 16,883
lines), declared in `Mojo/include/Mojo/KGENPasses.td`.

**F2. Modular does not use upstream's `DataFlowSolver` anywhere in the
open tree** (*measured*: `grep -rln 'DataFlowSolver\|dataflow::'` over
`Mojo/`, `Support/`, `max/`, `AsyncRT/` finds no file). It carries its
own `SCCP.cpp` (790 lines), `IPDF.cpp` (622), `Mem2Reg.cpp` (617),
`SROA.cpp` (791), `Canonicalizer.cpp` (525), `DeadArgumentElimination.cpp`
(695), `StackReuse.cpp` (803) in `Mojo/lib/Transforms`; F5, F6, F8 and
F15 say what each stands in for and how it keeps its state (`SROA.cpp`
was not read).

**F3. Its interprocedural solver layer is one CRTP template pair**
(*read*): `Mojo/include/Mojo/TransformUtils/CallGraphUtils.h:36-199`
(`CallGraphNodeBase<DerivedT, FuncT, CallT>`, `CallGraphBase<DerivedT,
NodeT>`: a `MapVector<FuncOpT, NodeT>` of nodes, callers and callsites
per node, built with `mlir::parallelForEach` under a per-node
`SmartRWMutex`) and `SCCUtils.h:26-175` (`SCCGraph<DerivedT, NodeT,
BottomUpRewrite>`: SCCs from `llvm::scc_begin`, a dependency count per
SCC, fork-join scheduling of leaf SCCs first, `doAnalysis(node) -> bool`
iterated within an SCC, `doRewrite(node)` bottom-up or queued). Users:
`IPDF.cpp:336-342`, `ArgPromotion.cpp:44-73`, `InferFunctionAttrs.cpp:47-53`,
`ResolveCompilerPromises.cpp:74-85` (SCCGraph); `DeadArgumentElimination`,
`AutomaticInline`, `EliminateDuplicateFunctions`, `InlineParametric`
(CallGraphBase). No virtual call: the derived class's methods are found
statically.

**F4. EliminateDuplicateFunctions, the original** (*read*,
`Mojo/lib/Transforms/EliminateDuplicateFunctions.cpp:31-301`): a
`DenseSet<FuncOp, DuplicateFuncOpEquivalenceInfo>` whose hash is the
attributes but the name plus `OperationEquivalence::computeHash` per body
op, whose equality is `isRegionEquivalentTo`; callees deduplicated before
callers by AsyncRT chains (`doneDedup` per node, `andThenAsyncMoving`
over its dependencies), cycles skipped, one `std::mutex` around the set,
then a parallel pass redirecting each call to the class's
lexicographically smallest symbol; duplicates left for a later DCE.
Its test states the property it guarantees, not an op sequence alone:
cascading merges across three levels of the call graph, and "regardless
of the parallel processing order, every equivalence class collapses to
its lexicographically smallest symbol" (*read*,
`Mojo/test/kgen/transforms/eliminate-duplicated-functions-multilevel.mlir:1-12`).
The callee-first order is what makes one pass transitive: a caller is
hashed only after its callees were redirected, so two callers that
differed only in which copy they called now hash alike.

**F5. Its own SCCP is a structured abstract interpreter, not a solver
over program points** (*read*, `Mojo/lib/Transforms/SCCP.cpp:39-790`).
It reuses upstream's lattice value classes (`Lattice<ConstantValue>`
from `mlir/Analysis/DataFlow/ConstantPropagationAnalysis.h`, line 22) and
upstream folding (`op->fold`, `OperationFolder`), but none of the solver:
state is one `DenseMap<Value, ConstantState *>` with a
`SpecificBumpPtrAllocator` (lines 47-62), filled by walking each
single-block region in order (`processRegion`, 585-650); a branch whose
condition is a known constant visits only the taken region
(`getEntryTargets` with constant operands, 316-375, the call at 333); a
loop is iterated from a queue of entry states up to a threshold, 5, or 2
below loop depth 2, or the trip count (`getLoopConvergeThreshold`,
292-305), and when the threshold is met its results become Unknown and
the pass continues (430-445): a budget that only weakens the result. Its
weakness: a nested region copies the whole map (`AnalysisStateType
nestedState = state`, 399, 454, 640), the copy-per-edge shape of our
b8301ebe wall; it is per function, and the description says so ("doesn't have
inter-procedural support (yet)", `KGENPasses.td:1160-1170`).

**F6. IPDF: a prototype that shows the honest-solver shape and a
compact dense state** (*read*, `Mojo/lib/Transforms/IPDF.cpp:28-622`).
The lattice is a `SmartVariant<BottomState, AliasState, TopState>` with
`llvm::CastInfo` specializations so `isa<TopState>(state)` reads like
MLIR (`State`, 77-110; `CastInfo`, 258-288; `join`, 290-316). The
dense memory state is "a poor man's functional data structure": a row
per op that *writes*, each row a vector of pointers to states shared
with the previous row, a new state allocated only for the slot written
(`RegionState`, 136-237, comment at 226-233). Unknown ops are read
through upstream's `MemoryEffectOpInterface`, effect by effect, with the
effect's value (449-475); an op without the interface is assumed to read
and write every operand (476-479). The pass writes a string attribute
(`ipdf`, 593) and stops at regions with `llvm_unreachable("not handled
yet")` (357): it is a sketch, but the state representation is the
lesson.

**F7. Its own CFG for region control flow, instead of
`RegionBranchOpInterface`** (*read*,
`Mojo/include/Mojo/HLCFDialect/HLCFInterfaces.td:19-96`,
`HLCFOps.td:37-49`, `Analysis/CFG.h:19-45`). HLCF's ops implement two
interfaces of Modular's: `ControlFlowNode::getEntryTargets(constant
operands) -> targets` and `ControlFlowTerminator::getBranchTargets(...)`
plus `isParentNode` (a terminator such as `hlcf.break` or `hlcf.return`
leaves several regions at once, a "control-flow tree",
`HLCFDialect.td:23-129`). No HLCF op implements
`RegionBranchOpInterface` or `LoopLikeOpInterface` (*measured*, grep of
`HLCFOps.td`). `CFGAnalysis` is two `DenseMap`s (predecessors per
`(node, region index)`, successors per terminator) filled in one walk:
each node and each terminator is asked once for its targets, and each
target's predecessor list is appended in the same step
(`lib/HLCFDialect/Analysis/CFG.cpp:20-53`). One query per terminator,
never a query per pair of terminator and successor.

**F8. Its other solvers, and what each stands in for** (*read*):
- `DeadArgumentElimination.cpp:53-695`: a port of LLVM's IPO pass
  (`RetOrArg`, `Live`/`MaybeLive`, a `std::multimap` of maybe-live
  dependencies, 75-120, `surveyFunction` 346, `propagateLiveness` 466),
  state per argument and result, not per value; the rewrite runs in
  `mlir::parallelForEach` per function (670-681). Upstream's
  `remove-dead-values` is not used (*measured*: no include of it).
- `Mem2Reg.cpp:14-617`: promotion over structured regions with a
  `llvm::ScopedHashTable` of current values, adding a loop-carried value
  through `ControlFlowNode::insertVariants` for each allocation stored
  inside a node; the comment at 60-65 states why the promotability check
  owns that bookkeeping ("recomputing it per node would mean re-testing
  every store against every node").
- `StackReuse.cpp:51-118`: a potential-value analysis whose header states
  what it does not do ("not at maximum strength because ... 1. ... 2.").
- `AutomaticInline.cpp:104-500`: bottom-up inlining over
  `CallGraphBase`, each caller waiting on its callees' AsyncRT chains, an
  inner function pipeline run on each function once its callees are done
  (`completeFunctionProcessing`, 380-412) from a per-thread
  `mlir::PassManager` (`InliningUtils.h:70-90`,
  `InliningUtils.cpp:285-318`), callees in the caller's SCC never
  inlined, the cost model `numOps < threshold` (370-373).
- `InferFunctionAttrs.cpp:40-151`: an `SCCGraph` client in about 100
  lines (`convergent` propagated bottom-up).

**F9. Upstream mechanisms it does use** (*measured*, files including each
header under `Mojo/` and `Support/`): `SymbolTableAnalysis` 28 (cached
across passes, `isInvalidated` returns false,
`.toolchain/llvm-project/mlir/include/mlir/Analysis/SymbolTableAnalysis.h:20-51`),
`mlir/IR/Threading.h` 12, `Dominance.h` 10, `SideEffectInterfaces.h` 8,
`FunctionInterfaces.h` 7, `DialectConversion.h` 6, `RegionUtils.h` 6,
`GreedyPatternRewriteDriver.h` 4, `ThreadLocalCache.h` 4,
`ControlFlowInterfaces.h` 4, `CallGraph.h` 1, `Liveness.h`,
`SliceAnalysis.h`, `AliasAnalysis.h`, `TopologicalSortUtils.h`, `CSE.h`,
SCF, Linalg and Bufferization 0. Its canonicalizer is upstream's greedy

**F10. One thread pool for everything** (*read*). The `MLIRContext`'s
pool is AsyncRT's CPU device behind `llvm::ThreadPoolInterface`
(`AsyncRT/include/AsyncRT/CompilerSupport/LLVMThreadPool.h:22-68`), so
`mlir::parallelForEach`, the pass manager and Modular's own solvers
(`ForkJoin`, `ForkJoin.h:22-66`; `andThenAsyncMoving` on chains) share
one work queue. Shared state is named by type: `Shared<T>` with
`read(fn)`/`modify(fn)` under an `RWMutex`
(`Support/include/Support/Threading/Shared.h:22-62`),
`ThreadLocalCache<CacheT>` copies merged by `consolidate`
(`ThreadLocalCache.h:24-70`), `ConcurrentAppendingVector` with lock-free
reads (`ADT/ConcurrentAppendingVector.h:31-155`). The elaborator puts a
thread-local front (`mlir::ThreadLocalCache`) before each `Shared` map
(`Mojo/lib/Elaborator/Elaborator.h:110-118,166-200,375-383`).

**F11. Its monomorphizer is demand-driven, concurrent and suspendable,
with no depth cap by default** (*read*, `Mojo/lib/Elaborator/Elaborator.h`,
`IREvaluator.h:150-262`, `Elaborator.cpp:700-730`). An instance is keyed
by `(ParameterExprArrayAttr, GeneratorOpInterface)`, the parameters a
uniqued attribute (`Elaborator.h:54-58`); each `ImplNode` holds a stack
of scopes and, when a callee is not ready, returns `skipNode` and waits
on the callee's async value (`InterpreterCache.h:102-131`,
`IREvaluator.h:200-210`). `maxDepth` defaults to
`std::numeric_limits<unsigned>::max()` (`KGENPasses.td:500-502` in
`ElaborateGenerators`, `Mojo/include/Mojo/ToolCommon/CompilationOptions.h:144,240`);
recursion is found by SCC (`diagnoseAndBreakRecursion`,
`Elaborator.h:350-355`).
Compile-time evaluation runs an interpreter over the IR
(`IREvaluator : BytecodeInterpreter`), its results cached per
`(FuncOp, ParameterExprArrayAttr)` and asserted deterministic across
threads (`Elaborator.h:166-200`).

**F13. C++ style** (*measured* over `Mojo/lib`, `Mojo/include`,

**F14. Its rules for agents enforce process, not code shape** (*read*).

**F15. Its largest per-function solver states its threading contract in
its types** (*read*, `Mojo/lib/LowerLIT/CheckLifetimes.cpp`, 5,729 lines:
Mojo's borrow checker and destructor insertion, "inserts destructor calls
after the last use of a value", `KGENPasses.td:123-137`). Three tiers:
`WholeProgramState`, maps "immutable after construction, populated once
before threading begins" (198-264); a `PerThreadCache` of type layouts
and origin results "reused across all functions processed by that
thread", reset between functions for determinism (808-823); per-function
state, tracked values numbered and given a bit range each over their
flattened fields (`ValueInfo`, 837-895). The driver is one call
(5610-5650): `M::failableParallelForEach(ctx, range, func, cache,
consolidate)`, which copies the cache once per *worker*, not per element,
runs on the context's `llvm::ThreadPoolTaskGroup` under a
`ParallelDiagnosticHandler`, and merges the copies at the end
(`Support/include/Support/Compiler/Threading.h:28-111`); symbol lookups go
through upstream's `LockedSymbolTableCollection`
(`.toolchain/llvm-project/mlir/include/mlir/IR/SymbolTable.h:341-351`).
Loops converge on a lattice whose bits only fall ("we know this will
terminate", 3338-3351): a measure, not a count. One place still asserts
a count instead (`assert(numIters < 5 && "Loop should converge in a
couple iterations")`, 5049).

**Mojo as a model of parameters, traits and monomorphization.**
F20 to F25 are from a reader this lane dispatched over `Mojo/docs`,
`Mojo/proposals`, `Mojo/stdlib` and `Mojo/lib/Elaborator`, spot-checked
by this lane where marked. `sources/docs/mojo` holds the same five
documents as `third_party/modular`, and its `SNAPSHOT.md`, which says
the compiler is not in the repository, is out of date: the compiler has
been open since about Mojo 1.0 (the date, 2026-08-11, is a web search
tool's summary of third-party issues, unverified; the tree itself is the
evidence).

**F20. Parameters are attributes; the frontend checks traits, KGEN
does not** (*read*). A parameter is a compile-time value of any type,
types included, held as an MLIR attribute, whose definitions follow
"some DAG ordering", not SSA dominance
(`Mojo/docs/compiler/manual/Rationale.md:78-105`). LIT, the parser's
dialect, knows `T: MyTrait`; in KGEN "every trait in Mojo lowers to
`!kgen.type`" and "`<x: type>` is exactly equivalent to
`template<typename T>` in C++. Like C++, KGEN's templates are
duck-typed" (`Mojo/docs/compiler/manual/Terminology.md:171-183`,
spot-checked). The gap costs it `rebind[...]`, a type equality deferred
to elaboration as an assertion, because a generic body is checked once
and not refined under `comptime if`
(`docs/site/manual/parameters/index.mdx:1348-1401`,
`stdlib/std/builtin/rebind.mojo:22-61`), and errors that arrive as
elaborator stack traces ("you get a stack trace out of the elaborator",
`proposals/where_clauses.md:69-79`).

**F21. Traits are nominal and resolved only at compile time; there is
no dynamic dispatch** (*read*). Conformance is explicit, at the type's
definition, with default methods, associated types and comptime
values (`docs/site/manual/traits.mdx:62-193`); composition `A & B` is a
union of constraint sets with no `A | B`
(`proposals/trait_composition.md:28-137`); conformance is conditional by
default in the stdlib (`List[T](Copyable where conforms_to(T,
Copyable))`, `stdlib/std/collections/list.mojo:157-169`); witness tables
exist only during elaboration and are built lazily, because "eagerly
elaborating all conformances will vastly increase the workload"
(`docs/compiler/arcana/WitnessElaboration.md:8-47`). Structs "don't
allow dynamic dispatch" and existentials are an unstarted roadmap item
(`docs/site/manual/basics.mdx:187-190`, `docs/site/roadmap.mdx:238-239`);
`Some[Trait]` is an inferred type parameter, not an existential
(`stdlib/std/builtin/anytype.mojo:51-77`); where code is shared, the
stdlib erases by hand, a pointer and a thin function pointer
(`stdlib/std/format/tstring.mojo:28-128`). `Variant` is a closed sum
over a type pack whose copy and destroy are a `comptime for` that
unrolls into a switch on the discriminant, its storage chosen by a type
function over conformances (`stdlib/std/utils/variant.mojo:279-436`).

**F22. Monomorphization is demand-driven from roots, keyed by an
interned tuple, deduplicated after** (*read*): roots are exported
parameterless generators (`Elaborator.cpp:3120-3135`); `comptime if`
elaborates the taken branch only; the key is `(ParameterExprArrayAttr,
GeneratorOpInterface)`, sugar stripped to canonical form when LIT is
lowered so key equality is pointer equality
(`Elaborator.cpp:1674-1693`, `docs/compiler/manual/ParameterSugar.md:247-320`);
duplicate bodies merged afterwards (F4). Before instantiating, it shrinks
generators, because "fewer, smaller, simpler generators are faster to
instantiate (e.g. better for elaborator caching)"
(`docs/compiler/MojoCompilerWalkthrough.md:311-312,421-425`), and its
parametric inliner works from the leaves because inlining from the roots
"splats callgraph into a tree" (`InlineParametric.cpp:354-366`,
spot-checked). Parallel elaboration replaced recursion in the compiler
by suspension: a node that meets a new instantiation is parked as a
waiter and requeued when it completes; state and waiter count share one
atomic; a cycle shows as an exhausted work queue with roots unfinished,
then a trimmed DFS breaks it or reports it
(`docs/compiler/overviews/ParallelElaboration.md:188-334`, read by this
lane). There is no instance cache across modules: packages hold
post-parse IR and elaboration happens in the importing program, whole
program (`MojoCompilerWalkthrough.md:612-645,800-812`).

**F23. Its limits mostly degrade, one refuses by count** (*read*):
instantiation depth is uncapped by default and, when set, refuses with
"elaborator expansion is N levels deep - infinite recursion?", a depth
that "varies based on the traversal order of the callgraph"
(`Elaborator.cpp:719-726`, `IREvaluatorContext.h:145-147`); the
interpreter's call depth has the same opt-in cap
(`Mojo/include/Mojo/Interpreter/ParametricInterpreterState.h:127-146`).
Everything else is a budget that leaves code unoptimized: speculative
symbolic folding stops at 32 nested interpretations, "an expression that
is left unevaluated" (`ParameterEvaluator.h:116-123`); the parametric
inliner caps at 5,000 ops and does not iterate cycles
(`InlineParametric.cpp:571-600,692-697`); unrolling a `comptime for`
past 65,536 iterations is a warning (`Elaborator.cpp:1313-1321`).

**F24. Proven facts it erases before monomorphizing** (*read*): origins
(lifetimes) are checked once on generic IR and erased before
elaboration, "no conditional logic that can depend on them, and
therefore no code specialization due to them"
(`proposals/origin-design.md:16-125`, lines 121-122 spot-checked);
traits become `!kgen.type` (F20). The compile-time interpreter evaluates
through folders and `InterpreterOpInterface::interpret` over emulated
memory, compiled to bytecode, cached per `(FuncOp, parameters)` with
determinism asserted (`docs/compiler/overviews/Interpreter.md:22-221`,
`Elaborator.h:166-196`). Library code enters the compiler's symbolic
algebra through `@always_inline("builtin")`, its body checked once for a
restricted form (`proposals/always_inline_builtin.md:41-159`), 222 uses
in the stdlib.

**F25. Its compiler documents its own design** (*read*):
`Mojo/docs/compiler/` holds a design overview (1,171 lines), a walkthrough
(1,412), rationale, parallel elaboration, the interpreter, witness
elaboration, generics, testing ("Mojo compiler team adds tests with
every change", lit and FileCheck, `testing.md:1-110`) and compile-time
profiling. Its transform tests are FileCheck over op sequences
(`Mojo/test/kgen/transforms/*.mlir`, 65 files, *measured*); the best of them state
the property in prose (F4).

#### 5.4.2 What it means for idris-mlir

All *conjecture* until wave 2 reads our code; each line maps findings
onto §2 and §3.

1. **§3's conjecture holds in industry** (F2, F5, F8, F15). The largest
   open MLIR compiler of a language runs no `DataFlowSolver`; it keeps
   upstream's lattice *values* (`ConstantValue`), folders, effects
   interface, `FunctionOpInterface`, dominance and greedy driver, and
   replaces only the *engine and its state* with per-problem solvers
   whose state is per function, per argument, or per tracked field. So
   the honest solver layer D2 asks for is the industrial norm, and its
   boundary is sharp: upstream's values, hooks and interfaces in;
   upstream's solver, program points and per-value lattices out.
2. **The solver layer already has a shape to copy** (F3, F8, F10). Our
   call-graph analyses (`CallCycles`, `Recursion`, `Effects`, `Inline`,
   the label solve's interprocedural part, `optimize`'s bottom-up
   inline-and-specialize, the deduplicator) are each "a summary per
   function, callees first". Modular runs all of these on one template,
   `SCCGraph`, whose composition and threading are visible in about 175
   lines: what an analysis reads (finished callees' summaries), writes
   (its node), and what may run beside it (any SCC not on its path).
   That is D2's "where how they compose and are threaded is clear",
   already written. It lacks a top-down direction (caller to callee,
   for constants and binding times at call sites) and its fixpoint
   inside an SCC asserts a count (`SCCUtils.h:76-77`) where AGENTS.md
   wants a measure.
3. **The deduplicator's port** (F4, `PINS.md` eliminate-duplicate-functions).
   Our port replaced AsyncRT's per-node chains by waves by height with a
   barrier per wave; Modular's per-node readiness never idles a thread
   on an unbalanced wave. With `SCCGraph` on MLIR's pool (design 1
   below) the port becomes one more client and the waves go.
4. **The `RegionBranchOpInterface` wall** (F7; §3, e96bb991). Modular's
   answer to "who branches where" is a table built once per function
   (`CFGAnalysis`: successors per terminator, predecessors per region
   inverted from them), each terminator asked once. Whatever interface
   we query (upstream's, patched, or one of ours), the quadratic shape
   goes when predecessors are inverted from successors in one pass and
   cached, instead of asked per successor.
5. **Dense state that does not copy** (F5, F6; §3, b8301ebe and
   ffa91113). Modular's own SCCP copies its whole map into each nested
   region (the shape of our 47 GB `idr-rc-stack` wall); its IPDF
   prototype shows the fix: a row of state pointers only at ops that
   write, shared with the row before, a state allocated only for the
   slot written. Counting, stack-cell inference and exclusivity keep
   state per program point; this representation is a candidate for all
   three.
6. **Dead arguments without whole-program liveness** (F8; §3,
   16005000). Modular's dead-argument pass keeps a liveness bit per
   argument and result and a map of "live if that is live"
   dependencies, LLVM's algorithm: state proportional to signatures,
   not to values.
7. **Threading by construction** (F10, F15; §3, 98c6315c). Modular names
   every shared structure by its sharing: immutable after construction,
   per worker (copied once per worker, merged at the end), per function;
   `Shared<T>` for a locked one, a thread-local front before it. It
   never reads a mutable fact out of a type: its instances are keyed by
   uniqued attributes. Our `!idr.data` is a mutable type whose facts were
   read under the context's lock; wave 2 should check whether the table
   as an immutable attribute, built once, plus a per-worker front, makes
   that wall unrepresentable.
8. **Specialization** (F11, F22, F23). Mojo's elaborator is our
   specializer's closest relative: lazy from roots, instance keyed by an
   interned (definition, arguments) pair, suspension instead of
   recursion, duplicates merged after, and only one limit that refuses,
   an opt-in depth that depends on traversal order. Our rules already
   forbid that one and keep the rest. Its erasures (traits to
   `!kgen.type`, origins before elaboration, F20, F24) are what we must
   not do: `!idr.lin<T>` and the grades survive specialization because
   in-place update needs them after it.
9. **Effects** (F12). Modular declares little: `Pure`, operand effects,
   and seven hand-written `getEffects` where an attribute (`volatile`,
   atomic ordering) changes what the op does, the flag the
   representation-first skill says to split. On effects it is not the
   exemplar; P0's one effects model should go further than Modular went.
10. **C++ style** (F13, F19). Modular is C++20 with no concepts; its
    templates are CRTP over op and interface types, `if constexpr`
    policies and compile-time tracing levels; its ladder density is
    ours. "Concept-heavy C++26" has no precedent here; the precedent is
    *where* it puts templates (graph schedules over op types, typed
    answers), which fits Q1's reading.
11. **Verification** (F16). Modular verifies after every pass only in
    development builds and puts explicit verifier passes at phase
    boundaries in production. AGENTS.md requires the grade verifier
    after every pass; at 71 MB the cost question is real (Q-M2).
12. **Rules for agents** (F14). Modular's rules govern process (a
    trailer, small PRs, human-written descriptions, a CI-checked title)
    and leave code shape to review; ours govern design. What transfers is
    mechanical enforcement: `-Werror` in CI, clang-tidy as a test with
    warnings as errors, a lint for titles. Its 100-line PR rule does not
    fit a hard cutover (D4 and §1).
13. **Testing** (F4, F25). FileCheck over op sequences, with the property
    in prose at best: weaker than our testing rule; not a model.

#### 5.4.3 Candidate designs, ranked

Ranked by what each deletes and how many of §3's walls it makes
unrepresentable. Sizes at §3's scale are *conjecture* (70,000 functions;
call edges unmeasured, taken as ten per function).

**Design 1. One call-graph schedule for every summary analysis: a port
of `SCCGraph`, both directions, on MLIR's pool.** The solver layer's
spine. A problem is a type, checked by a concept (our own C++, Q1's
line); the schedule is one function template; the graph is built once
and cached through the `AnalysisManager`.

```cpp
// Solver/Schedule.h: the order in which summaries are computed, and the
// one place that says which runs beside which.
enum class Changed : bool { no, yes };
enum class Direction { calleesFirst, callersFirst };

template <class P>
concept SummaryProblem =
    requires(P &p, const CallGraph &g, FunctionId f) {
      typename P::Summary;                       // the fact per function
      { p.analyze(g, f) } -> std::same_as<Changed>;
      { p.rewrite(g, f) } -> std::same_as<void>;
    };

// An SCC is enqueued when its last neighbour SCC (callees, or callers)
// finishes; analyze(f) reads only finished neighbours' summaries and
// writes only f's, so no summary is ever locked. Inside an SCC a
// worklist runs until P's lattice stops rising (P names the measure).
template <SummaryProblem P, Direction D>
void solve(MLIRContext &ctx, const CallGraph &g, P &problem);
```

`CallGraph` here is dense: `FunctionId` a 32-bit index in module order,
callees and callers as two compressed rows (offsets and targets), SCCs in
post order; upstream's `mlir::CallGraph` if wave 2 and the mlir-analysis
lane find its cost at 70,000 functions acceptable, since only the
*schedule* is missing upstream (its inliner visits SCCs one after
another, `.toolchain/llvm-project/mlir/lib/Transforms/Utils/Inliner.cpp:285-299`,
and is parallel only inside one SCC, 527-560). Buys: every
interprocedural analysis states its reads, writes and neighbours by
construction; callees-first and callers-first are one parameter (Modular
has only the first); parallel across the whole SCC DAG; a measure, not
`assert(!changed && "self recursion didn't converge in 2 iterations")`.
Costs: about 6 MB of graph (two rows of 32-bit indices each way), one
task per SCC, one atomic count per SCC; no lock per node (Modular's
build takes a writer lock on two nodes per edge,
`CallGraphUtils.h:184-191`). Deletes (*conjecture*, wave 2 confirms):
each analysis's own SCC order (`lib/Support/Scc.cpp`, `CallCycles`,
`Recursion`), the deduplicator's waves, `optimize`'s own bottom-up
scheduling. Licence: a port keeps Modular's header and gets a `PINS.md`
entry like the deduplicator's; a fresh implementation informed by it
needs neither.

**Design 2. Per-function solvers on one parallel driver with a stated
three-tier contract.** Modular's
`failableParallelForEach(ctx, range, func, cache, consolidate)` (F15)
with its three tiers written in each solver's header: immutable
whole-program inputs, a cache copied once per worker and merged at the
end, per-function state dropped after the function. Upstream first: the
per-worker overload is small and generic, a natural patch to
`mlir/include/mlir/IR/Threading.h`; until then it lives in the solver
layer. Buys: a function pass's parallelism without a lock on a hot path
(§4's "threaded by construction"), deterministic output (each worker
cache reset per function, as CheckLifetimes does). Deletes: ad hoc
`std::mutex`es around shared caches (§2 counts 22 mutex or atomic uses).

**Design 3. Dense per-point state as rows shared until written.** For
the solvers that keep a fact per program point (stack-cell inference,
exclusivity, borrows): IPDF's `RegionState` (F6), a row only at an op
that writes, each row pointers into the previous row's states, a new
state only for the slot written; slots numbered densely per function.
Buys: memory proportional to writes, not to ops times slots; removes the
copy along every edge that b8301ebe hit and that Modular's own SCCP
still does (F5). Costs: one pointer per slot per writing op. Deletes:
per-value candidate vectors copied along edges (wave 2 confirms where
any survive).

**Design 4. Region control flow as a table built once.** Successors per
terminator, predecessors per (op, region) inverted from them in one pass,
cached per function (`CFGAnalysis`, F7). Whether the per-terminator query
is upstream's `RegionBranchTerminatorOpInterface` (preferred, patched if
needed) or an interface of ours is the mlir-analysis and mlir-ods lanes'
call; the table is what removes the per-(terminator, successor) query of
e96bb991. Costs: two small vectors per region-holding op.

**Design 5. Instances keyed by interned arguments, cached in two tiers.**
For specialization and every cache across threads: key = (function,
uniqued attribute of its static arguments), equality by pointer (F11,
F22); a locked shared map with a per-worker front (`Shared<T>` plus
`mlir::ThreadLocalCache`, F10). Facts read by many threads live in
immutable attributes built once, never in a mutable type. Buys: 98c6315c
unrepresentable. Wave 2 decides whether our specializer should also
become demand-driven from roots with suspension (Mojo's elaborator) or
stay "each function once, bottom-up" (design 1's schedule).

**Design 6. Dead arguments and results as a summary problem.** LLVM's
algorithm as Modular ported it (F8): a bit per argument and result, a map
of "live if that is live", run as a design 1 client, rewriting
signatures and calls in parallel. Costs: proportional to signatures.
Deletes: any remaining whole-program value liveness used for dead
arguments.

**Design 7. Answers that carry what was learned.** In the solver layer's
API: `Changed` as an enum, three-way answers with evidence per state
(`TriResult`, F19), named states for suspendable work (`ElaborationState`),
never a `bool` with a side output. Cheap; it is the representation-first
skill applied to our own C++.

**Design 8. Mechanical enforcement in the build.** From Modular's
toolchain (F13): `-Wthread-safety` with real annotations, which Modular
turns on but never writes (no `guarded_by` anywhere, *measured*); the
pinned libc++ marks `std::mutex` a capability
(`.toolchain/llvm-project/libcxx/include/__mutex/mutex.h:25`,
`__configuration/attributes.h:318-322`), while LLVM's `RWMutex` on Apple
is its own unannotated implementation (`llvm/include/llvm/Support/RWMutex.h:22-24`);
`-Wcovered-switch-default`, `-Wsuggest-override`,
`-Werror=global-constructors`; clang-tidy run as a test with warnings
as errors.

**Design 9. `idris-mlir-tblgen` as parse-once.** Records parsed into a
typed model and validated with TableGen errors before any generator runs,
the errors tested (F18); optionally a checksum of the `.td` records
compiled into both the compiler and the generated Idris mirror, so two
artifacts from different records are refused when they meet. One fact
stays one place; the checksum only detects a stale copy.

**Not to take.** `SmartVariant`'s `CastInfo` over our own sums (it
brings `isa<>` ladders to closed sets, where `std::visit` is checked for
exhaustiveness); AsyncRT (a runtime dependency; MLIR's pool and
`llvm::ThreadPoolTaskGroup` do the same); a hand-written `getEffects`
where an attribute changes the effect; any option that selects between
two implementations (F17); turning the verifier off by default (Q-M2);
an optional depth cap (F23).
