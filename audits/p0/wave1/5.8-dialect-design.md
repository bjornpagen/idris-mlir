# 5.8 dialect-design: the idiomatic MLIR dialect, for a total redesign of `idr`

The `idr` dialect is a historical accident: it grew one case at a time.
P0 redesigns it whole: every op, type, attribute, effect, trait,
interface and name, renamed and merged where the representation says so.
This lane builds the knowledge that redesign needs. It goes broad and
deep, and studies designs, not only mechanisms.
- **The type system first.** How idiomatic dialects design their types:
  parameters and what belongs in a type against an attribute or an op;
  type syntax and aliases; how upstream and Mojo carry qualifiers
  (ownership, mutability, address spaces, element types, shapes, lifetimes)
  in types (`memref`'s layout and memory space, `tensor`'s encoding,
  `!llvm.ptr`'s address space, `gpu` and `nvgpu` types, `async` tokens,
  `!transform` handles, `emitc`, `ptr`); type interfaces as the
  classification of types; mutable and recursive types and what it costs
  to have one; what a graded type such as `!idr.q<...>` should look like
  in that light.
- **Op granularity and naming.** One op per semantics, and where upstream
  draws the line between one op with an attribute and two ops; op
  families (`arith`'s signed and unsigned pairs, `memref.alloc`,
  `alloca`, `realloc`, `linalg`'s generated named ops, `vector`'s
  transfers); ops that are secretly one op with different effects, and
  how upstream makes the difference a type, an operand, an interface or
  an effect instead. Mnemonic conventions, the use of dots, prefixes,
  verbs and nouns, and the custom assembly syntax of exemplary dialects.
- **Effects as design.** How dialects model effects on state they own
  (`async`, `gpu`, `omp`, `memref`, `llvm`, `transform`'s handle
  consumption, `bufferization`), resources, tokens and chains against
  effects, and what that implies for our world token, our IO, our
  references and our crashes.
- **Mojo's higher-level IR.** Every dialect Modular's tree and docs
  describe (KGEN, POP, LIT and the rest, as they exist in
  `third_party/modular` and `sources/docs/mojo`): its types (parametric
  types, references, origins and lifetimes, ownership conventions such as
  owned, borrowed and inout), its ops, its effects, how it carries
  compile-time parameters and when it monomorphizes, its syntax.
- **MLIR's own guidance**, read in full: the language reference, the
  ODS, attribute and type, interface, traits, side-effects, canonicalization,
  symbols, dialect conversion, bufferization and
  "defining dialects" documents, the developer guide, the rationale
  documents (generic DAG rewriter, MLIR rationale, usage of `const`), the
  MLIR papers (CGO 2021, the 2020 preprint) and talks on dialect design;
  the Discourse threads on dialect design where they settle a question.
- **Exemplary dialects to study as designs:** `arith`, `scf`, `cf`,
  `func`, `memref`, `tensor`, `linalg`, `vector`, `llvm`, `async`,
  `gpu`, `transform`, `emitc`, `ptr`, `ub`, `bufferization`, `index`,
  `math`, and outside the tree CIRCT's `hw`, `comb`, `seq` and `firrtl`,
  ClangIR's `cir`, Flang's `fir` and `hlfir`, Rust and Swift's SIL where
  `sources/` holds them (`sources/docs/swift`).
- **The output:** a design guide for the redesign (naming rules, the
  type system, op granularity, effects, syntax), each rule grounded in
  the documents and exemplars above, and a first mapping of §2's surface
  onto it: which of our ops look like one op, which types should carry
  what, which names change. The mapping is *conjecture* until wave 2
  reads our code.

**Summary.** (1) In MLIR a value's type is the only per-value fact every
pass keeps, so AGENTS.md's grades-in-types rule is MLIR's grain, not a
workaround; the idiomatic form is one grade *attribute* inside the type,
implementing an interface that verifiers ask, normalized so the
unrestricted grade is the bare type (B6, T1-T4, DS1). (2) Upstream's line
between one op and several: one op per computation, an enum only where
variants share structure, traits, effects and verification (B3, B4, H1);
ops differing only in effect are one TableGen class parameterized by the
resource (C3). (3) Consumption is an effect on the value (`Read` +
`Free`), as the transform dialect consumes handles (C4). (4) Idiomatic
MLIR orders IO with effects on a resource tree, not tokens; the builtin
`token` cannot carry a world (B11, EF4, DS2). (5) Mojo keeps lifetimes and
conventions in types and function types until one checking pass, then
erases them in one step (A2-A4, G6).

#### 5.8.1 Findings

**A. Mojo's higher-level IR (LIT, KGEN, POP and kin) is in the tree.**

A1. *(read)* `third_party/modular` carries the `.td` sources of Mojo's
own dialects, about 27,000 lines: `Mojo/include/Mojo/LITDialect`
(the language-level IR: `LITTypes.td` 715 lines, `LITOps.td` 2,197,
`LITAttrs.td` 1,029), `KGENDialect` (the parametric "kernel generator"
IR: `KGENTypes.td` 1,270, `KGENOps.td` 1,772, `KGENAttrs.td` 3,600),
`POPDialect` (parametric operations, `POPOps.td` 2,615), `HLCFDialect`
(high-level control flow), `CODialect` (coroutines), `DepGraphDialect`,
and `Support/include/Support/MDialect`. The Mojo stdlib's
`CLAUDE.md` calls `pop`, `kgen` and `lit` private APIs
(`Mojo/CLAUDE.md`, "Internal APIs").

A2. *(read)* **A reference carries its lifetime and mutability in its
type, as attributes inside the type.** `!lit.ref<T, origin, addrspace>`
(`Mojo/include/Mojo/LITDialect/LITTypes.td:477-562`): `elementType` is a
type parameter, `origin` a `TypedAttr` parameter, `addressSpace` a
defaulted `TypedAttr` (`:494-496`). Mutability is not a third flag on the
reference: it is the *type of the origin*, `!lit.origin<isMutable>`
(`:409-450`), where `isMutable` is itself a `TypedAttr`, so it may be a
constant or a compile-time parameter (`MutabilityClass {Mutable,
Immutable, Parametric}`, `:436-441`). Printed: `!lit.ref<f32,
some_origin>`, `!lit.ref<f32, mut=sometimes_mutable, some_origin, 0>`
(`:486-490`). The description states the phase: "This is lowered to a
!kgen.pointer after CheckLifetimes verifies that the uses of it are all
correct" (`:481-483`); `lit.ref.to_kgen_ptr` says "Origin information is
discarded" (`LITOps.td:1808-1837`).

A3. *(read)* **Origins are an algebra of uniqued attributes, normalized
on construction.** `#lit.any.origin`, `#lit.static.origin`,
`#lit.comptime.origin` (singletons per mutability), `#lit.origin.union`
("canonicalized on construction"), `#lit.origin.field<base, field>`
(field-sensitive: "known to alias the larger origin, but not other
sibling origins"), `#lit.interior.origin`, `#lit.origin.subtree<x>`,
`#lit.origin.mutcast` ("often folded away by the builder"),
`#lit.origin.set<{mut a, imm b, (is_mut) c}>`
(`LITAttrs.td:378-830`). Every one is a `TypedAttr` whose type is
`!lit.origin<mut>` (`AttributeSelfTypeParameter<"", "OriginType">`), and
their builders "fold the request automatically" (`:526-531`). So the
lattice operations of lifetime checking (join, projection, widening) are
attribute constructors, and two references with equal origins have
pointer-equal types.

A4. *(read)* **Calling conventions and function effects live in the
function type.** `!kgen.func` has parameters `FunctionType values`,
`FnMetadataAttr metadataAttr`, `PogListAttr argListAttrs`; "The metadata
attribute holds the argument calling conventions, the effects of the
function itself, and the dialect-specific function metadata"
(`Mojo/include/Mojo/KGENDialect/KGENTypes.td:353-410`). The conventions
are one enum per argument, `ArgConvention`: `imm` (borrowed in a
register), `imm_mem`, `owned`, `owned_in_mem`, `deinit_mem`, `mut`,
`ref`, `mutref` ("guaranteed to always be mutable, and thus are known to
be no-alias"), `byref_result`, `byref_error` (`KGENEnums.td:191-264`);
the effects a bit enum `FnEffects {Throws, Async, Capturing, RefResult,
CABI}` (`:266-285`). A call's legality and a callee's obligations are
read off the callee's type, never off a discardable attribute.

A5. *(read)* **Ownership ops in LIT are few and name one effect each.**
`lit.load.consume` ("consumes the ownership of the memory being
accessed", lowered to `pop.load`; `LITOps.td:1523-1546`) is a separate
op from `lit.ref.load` (`:1961-1983`), which takes a mutable or
immutable reference alike: mutability is read from the operand's type,
and `lit.ref.store` requires a mutable one (`:1985-2006`). The checker's
facts are ops of their own: `lit.ownership.use`, `.mark_initialized`,
`.mark_consumed`, `.mark_destroyed` (`:1574-1661`), all removed by
`LowerLIT`. Type-changing views are ops whose result type is computed
from the operand's: `lit.ref.immut` (`TypesMatchWith ...
getWithMutability(false)`, `:1663-1700`), `lit.ref.upcast` (widen an
origin, `:1702-1729`). Not all of LIT is exemplary: `lit.fn` carries
about 30 attributes, many `UnitAttr` flags (`:52-185`), and
`lit.ref.struct.ger` takes an optional field name *or* an optional
index (`:1870-1959`), which is a flag by §4's measure.

**B. MLIR's own guidance on what goes where.**

B1. *(read)* **Strict type equality, no dependent types.** "MLIR enforces
strict type equality checking and does not provide type conversion
rules"; "MLIR only supports non-dependent types, including trivial,
parametric, function, sum and product types" (`sources/papers/
lattner-2020-mlir/ir_design.tex:188-200`). Consequence for any qualifier
held in a type: every change of qualifier is an op, and every op that
accepts "any qualifier" says so in its operand constraint.

B2. *(read)* **No SSA values inside types.** "The current MLIR disallows
use of symbols in types ... An alternative design is to embed the
reference to symbols directly in the type - memref<8x%Nxf32>. We went for
the current approach in MLIR because it simplifies the design --- types
remain immutable when the values of symbols change"
(`docs/Rationale/Rationale.md:130-168`). A dynamic quantity is `?` in the
type and an operand on the op (`memref.alloc(%N) : memref<8x?xf32>`).
Mojo keeps its origins inside types for the same reason as attributes,
never as SSA values (A2, A3).

B3. *(read)* **Same computation, same op; sign is on the op, not the
type.** "it is a good thing for an intermediate representation to
represent the same computation with the same instruction. Signed types
got in the way, because (e.g.) an 'add of an sbyte' does the same
computation as an 'add of a ubyte', but the type system made them look
artificially different. This split also required casts like 'cast from
sbyte to ubyte' which do nothing at the machine level"
(`Rationale.md:241-281`). So `arith.addi` is one op, `arith.divsi` and
`arith.divui` two. The same document limits the rule to dialects that
"can express an opinion": "Other dialects generally try to model an
external system, and should aim to reflect its design as closely as
possible" (`:278-281`), which is why `ptr.load` and `llvm.load` carry
`volatile`, `nontemporal` and atomic-ordering flags as properties
(`include/mlir/Dialect/Ptr/IR/PtrOps.td:207-269`): they mirror LLVM.

B4. *(read)* **When one op takes an enum.** `arith.cmpi`'s predicate is an
attribute because comparisons "share several common properties" and are
"sometimes implemented by the same instruction or its variants";
"ten distinct operators ... would have increased the number of
'reserved' names ... as well as the size of the C++ API while their
implementations would have been mostly identical"; the predicate is an
integer enum, never a string, so "switching logic based on the
comparison kind" and validity checks stay cheap (`Rationale.md:312-349`).
Read together with B3, upstream's line is *(this lane's synthesis)*: *one op when the variants
share operand and result structure, traits, effects and verification and
differ only in the pure function computed; separate ops when the
computation, the effects, the undefined behaviour or the valid states
differ* (`arith.divsi`/`divui`: different results; `arith.addf`/`addi`:
NaN, rounding and fast-math, `:283-310`).

B5. *(read)* **Defaults are normalized away at construction.** "Identity
layout maps do not contribute to the MemRef type identification and are
discarded on construction. That is, a type with an explicit identity map
... is strictly the same as the one without a layout"
(`include/mlir/IR/BuiltinTypes.td:730-800`, the memref description).
Mojo's origin attributes do the same (A3), and `!lit.trait` drops
trivially true constraints in its builder
(`third_party/modular/Mojo/include/Mojo/LITDialect/LITTypes.td:286-296`).
One meaning, one uniqued type.

B6. *(read)* **Qualifiers are attributes that implement an interface, and
ops ask them.** `!ptr.ptr<#memory_space>` has one parameter of type
`MemorySpaceAttrInterface` (`include/mlir/Dialect/Ptr/IR/PtrDialect.td:
50-78`), whose methods `isValidLoad`, `isValidStore`, `isValidAtomicOp`,
`isValidAtomicXchg`, `isValidAddrSpaceCast`, `isValidPtrIntCast` decide
legality ("this interface allows concepts such as read-only memory to be
adequately modeled and enforced", `MemorySpaceInterfaces.td:9-130`).
`ptr.load` is one op; its verifier calls `ms.isValidLoad(...)`
(`lib/Dialect/Ptr/IR/PtrDialect.cpp:120-133, 187, 221, 252`). Likewise
memref's layout is any attribute implementing `MemRefLayoutAttrInterface`
and tensor's `encoding` is an attribute whose "semantics are defined by a
type and attribute interface and must be respected by all passes"
(`BuiltinTypes.td:1143-1180`). The qualifier's algebra lives with the
qualifier, not in each op.

B7. *(read)* **Type traits and type interfaces classify.** Tensor carries
the `ValueSemantics` type trait (`BuiltinTypes.td:39-42, 1143-1144`);
memref implements `PtrLikeTypeInterface` and `ShapedTypeInterface`
(`:730-733`). Bufferization admits dialect types through two type
interfaces, `TensorLikeType` (with `verifyCompatibleBufferType`) and
`BufferLikeType` ("used by types to opt into being supported by the
bufferization procedures", `include/mlir/Dialect/Bufferization/IR/
BufferizationTypeInterfaces.td`). The transform dialect's handle types
implement `TransformHandleTypeInterface`, `TransformParamTypeInterface`
or `TransformValueHandleTypeInterface`, each with `checkPayload`
(`include/mlir/Dialect/Transform/Interfaces/TransformInterfaces.td:
182-251`). A type's role is a trait or interface on the type, and an op's
operand constraint names the interface (`Transform_AnyHandleType`,
`:244-251`).

B8. *(read)* **Mutable types are the exception.** "Mutable parameters
should be reserved for parameters that can not be reasonably initialized
during construction time. Given the mutable component, these parameters
do not take part in the uniquing"; they are not expressible in ODS
(`docs/DefiningDialects/AttributesAndTypes.md:1084-1095`). The upstream
exemplar is LLVM's identified struct: "uniqued using their name";
constructed uninitialized, then "the body must be set up by a later call,
using MLIR's type mutation mechanism"; "Once the type is initialized, its
body cannot be changed anymore"; "if a concurrent thread initializes the
type before the current thread, the initialization may return failure"
(`docs/Dialects/LLVM.md:350-424`). New at the pin: "the context also
supports *transient scopes* ... Storage instances allocated while in a
transient scope are reclaimed when the scope ends"
(`AttributesAndTypes.md:997-1003`).

B9. *(read)* **What a function pass may read decides where a type's
definition can live.** A pass "Must not inspect the state of operations
that are siblings of the current operation ... Inspecting the state of
ancestor/parent operations is permitted" (`docs/PassManagement.md:
29-31`). A type whose body lives in a sibling declaration op (Mojo's
`!lit.struct<@MyStruct<...>>`, whose `value` is a `SymbolAttr` naming a
`lit.struct.decl`, `LITTypes.td:188-258`) cannot be read by a function
pass running in parallel; a body held in the type, or in an attribute on
an ancestor, can.

B10. *(read)* **Verifiers are local; whole-function invariants are
passes.** "only verify local aspects of an operation, in particular don't
follow def-use chains (don't look at the producer of any operand or the
user of any results)"; "Passes should not check invariants that are
guaranteed by the verifier"; "If a pass produces IR that fails the
verifier then the pass has a bug" (mlir.llvm.org/getting_started/
DeveloperGuide, "IR Verifier", "IR should be valid before and after each
pass"). The transform dialect checks use-after-consume of its handles in
a separate pass (`transform-dialect-check-uses`) and an opt-in expensive
check, not in op verifiers (`docs/Dialects/Transform.md:326-334`).

B11. *(read)* **Builtin `token` is not a world token.** New at the pin: a
`token` "is a pointer to an operation ... A token cannot be forwarded: a
token def-use chain cannot be obscured by ops with forwarding semantics";
it may not be "a forwarded result or operand of a `CallOpInterface` op",
"an argument or result type of a `FunctionOpInterface` op", "a successor
operand", "a forwarded operand to or from any region of a
`RegionBranchOpInterface` op"; `AnyType` excludes it (`docs/Tokens.md:
7-86`; `docs/LangRef.md:750-776`). It models LLVM-style "this came from
that op" links, not a state threaded through calls and loops.

B12. *(read)* **Progressive lowering, forms, and no raising.** The MLIR
paper: "Maintain higher-level semantics ... The loss of structure is then
conscious and happens only where the structure is no longer needed"
(`lattner-2020-mlir/design.tex:73-94`). Linalg names four
interchangeable forms of one meaning, *generic* (`linalg.generic`),
*category* (`linalg.contract`, `linalg.elementwise <kind>`), *named*
(`linalg.matmul`, `linalg.add`) and *composite*, and lets each transform
pick the form that is canonical for it ("a `matmul` operation is a
special case of a `contract` operation, which in turn is a special case
of a `generic` operation", `docs/Rationale/RationaleLinalgDialect.md:
509-573`); `linalg.elementwise`'s `kind` concatenates the unary, binary
and ternary function enums into one (`include/mlir/Dialect/Linalg/IR/
LinalgEnums.td:93-115`, `LinalgStructuredOps.td:550-620`). "The Dialect
Need not be Closed Under Transformations" (`:648-674`).

**C. Effects as design upstream.**

C1. *(read)* **Four kinds of implicit behaviour, two interfaces.** Memory
effects; undefined behaviour; non-termination; non-local control flow.
"`MemoryEffectsOpInterface` ... is used to track memory effects. The
`ConditionallySpeculatable` op interface is used to track undefined
behavior and infinite loops"; "Does it have side effects that must be
preserved, like a volatile store or a syscall? It should probably ...
model the effect as a read from or write to an abstract `Resource`";
"Can it infinitely loop on certain inputs? It should probably implement
`ConditionallySpeculatable`" (`docs/Rationale/SideEffectsAndSpeculation.
md:27-121`). Non-addressable resources "do not alias with any
value-based memory location"; the hierarchy is "deliberately *not*
intended for fine-grained regions with specific addresses" (`:78-96`).

C2. *(read)* **Totality is a trait, not an op.** `arith`'s total integer
ops derive from `Arith_TotalIntBinaryOp` (adds `Pure`); `arith.divui`
derives from the plain class with `ConditionallySpeculatable` ("Division
by zero is undefined behavior") (`include/mlir/Dialect/Arith/IR/
ArithOps.td:58-67, 596-612`). Refinements that only add poison
conditions (`exact`, `nsw`/`nuw` as `IntegerOverflowFlags`) are
properties on the same op (`:193-216`).

C3. *(read)* **Heap against stack is the resource, not a second op
family.** `memref.alloc` and `memref.alloca` are one TableGen class,
`AllocLikeOp<mnemonic, Resource>`, whose result is
`Res<AnyMemRef, "", [MemAlloc<resource, 0, FullEffect>]>`; `alloc` passes
`DefaultResource`, `alloca` passes `AutomaticAllocationScopeResource`
(`include/mlir/Dialect/MemRef/IR/MemRefOps.td:65-80, 227, 377`), a
subresource of the default (`include/mlir/Interfaces/
SideEffectInterfaces.h:198-199`). Two mnemonics, one definition: the
difference is exactly the effect.

C4. *(read)* **Consumption is an effect on the value.** The transform
dialect: consuming a handle is `Read` + `Free` on the operand, producing
one is `Allocate` + `Write` on the result, all on a non-addressable
`TransformMappingResource` (`lib/Dialect/Transform/Interfaces/
TransformInterfaces.cpp:1793-1852`; `include/.../TransformInterfaces.h:
1276-1290`); "using a value after it was freed is incorrect"
(`docs/Dialects/Transform.md:210-226`). `FunctionalStyleTransformOpTrait`
declares "consumes every operand, produces every result" once
(`TransformInterfaces.h:1334-1336`). A consuming op is therefore never
trivially dead, which is the right answer for a linear value.

C5. *(read)* **Tokens order asynchrony, effects order memory.** A
`!gpu.async.token` op "doesn't start executing until all depent ops
producing the async dependency tokens have finished"; if it returns a
token "the op merely schedules the execution ... If the op does not
return a token, the op will wait" (`include/mlir/Dialect/GPU/IR/
GPUBase.td:196-237`). The same ops keep their memory effects; the token
is an extra ordering edge across devices, read through
`GPU_AsyncOpInterface`. (The optional result that switches between
"schedule" and "wait" is a flag by §4's measure; upstream accepted it.)

C6. *(read)* **An abort is a write.** `cf.assert` declares only
`MemoryEffects::Write` on the default resource
(`lib/Dialect/ControlFlow/IR/ControlFlowOps.cpp:90-94`); `ub.unreachable`
is undefined behaviour, a terminator with no effects
(`include/mlir/Dialect/UB/IR/UBOps.td:73-88`); `ub.poison` is
`ConstantLike, Pure`. The write on the whole default resource is the
conservative way to keep an abort ordered after every observable effect.

C7. *(read)* **Ownership as a runtime value, decided per function.**
Ownership-based deallocation: "Ownership materializes as an `i1` SSA
value and can be thought of as 'responsibility to deallocate'"; it
"processes operations implementing `FunctionOpInterface` one-by-one
without analysing the call-graph", with an ABI ("When a MemRef is passed
as a function argument, ownership is never acquired"; "Returning a MemRef
... always passes ownership to the caller") and an
`uninitialized < unique(X) < unknown` lattice (`docs/
OwnershipBasedBufferDeallocation.md:1-130`).

**D. Swift SIL and OSSA** (read through a helper over
`sources/docs/swift`, `swift-evolution`; paths below are under
`sources/docs/swift/docs/SIL/` unless they say otherwise).

D1. *(read)* **Ownership is on the value, not the type.** Each value has
one kind, `None`, `Owned`, `Guaranteed` or `Unowned`
(`Ownership.md:8-22, 106-118`); "Owned values are consumed exactly once
along all paths" (`:172-176`); trivial values and addresses are `None`
(`:168-171`). The kind comes from a block argument's annotation
(`bb1(%0 : @owned $String, %1 : @guaranteed $String, %2 : $Int)`), from
the defining instruction (`copy_value` gives owned, `begin_borrow`
guaranteed), or is forwarded from operands (`SIL.md:486-513`). Each
operand carries (a kind constraint, a lifetime-ending bit), checked by
one join rule (`Ownership.md:120-151`).

D2. *(read)* **Forwarding is a category.** An `@owned` operand of a
forwarding instruction is consumed and its result is a new owned value; a
`@guaranteed` operand gives a guaranteed result in an implicit borrow
scope, so "one should never see end_borrows on any guaranteed forwarded
results" (`Ownership.md:423-493`). "owned values can never be partially
consumed": `struct_extract` cannot forward `@owned`; consuming an
aggregate takes `destructure_struct` (`:495-539`). Aggregate operands
must agree in kind (`SIL.md:503-513`).

D3. *(read)* **Qualifiers on one instruction where each expands to a
defined sequence.** `load [take]`/`[copy]`/`[trivial]`, `store
[init]`/`[assign]`/`[trivial]`, `copy_addr [take] ... [init]`, each
specified by its expansion into loads, stores, retains and releases
(`Instructions.md:821-871, 1252-1280`). Distinct instructions are grouped
by protocol "so that passes can deal with the group instead of individual
instruction opcodes" (`SIL-Utilities.md:192-195`). Reference counting is
staged: `strong_retain`/`release` are "_not_ available in OSSA" and are
lowered from `copy_value`/`destroy_value` (`Instructions.md:1650-1691,
3001, 3219`). "OSSA is 'lowered' to plain SSA. From this point on no
ownership verification can be done anymore" (`SIL.md:66-69`).

D4. *(read)* **Conventions are in the function type; lifetimes are
scopes.** The lowered type `(Int, @guaranteed String, @in_guaranteed any
P) -> ()` "adds information about the calling convention" (`SIL.md:
120-134`); `@moveOnly` "never shows up in a SILFunctionType's parameters
... a local power tool ... rather than a viral type level annotation"
(`Ownership.md:1021-1026`). Lifetimes are operands and scopes, never type
parameters: `mark_dependence %value on %base`, `begin_borrow`/
`end_borrow`, `begin_access [read|modify|init|deinit]
[static|dynamic]`/`end_access` (`Instructions.md:1521-1597, 1981-2044`).
Copy-on-write mutation makes a new SSA value: `begin_cow_mutation`
consumes its operand and returns `(%uniq, %buffer)` because "it's
important to generate a *new* buffer reference in SIL" (`SIL.md:
1565-1593`). Ownership on a trivial type makes it non-trivial
(`Ownership.md:1029`), and Swift erases the wrapper after checking.

D5. *(read)* **What Swift cannot do that we can.** "The ownership
convention becomes part of the ABI ... The optimizer also does not try to
optimize polymorphic interfaces" (`sources/docs/swift-evolution/
proposals/0377-parameter-ownership-modifiers.md:50-58, 333-336`). A
closed, monomorphic program has neither constraint. (Rust MIR's places
and `Operand::Copy`/`Move` are not in `sources/`: the helper found only
`rustc-dev-guide/src/query.md` and the monomorphization chapter.)

**F. Flang's FIR and HLFIR, and ClangIR** (read through a helper; paths
under `.toolchain/llvm-project/`).

F1. *(read)* **A qualifier that must hold at every use is a type; the
rest are attributes on the declaring op.** FIR keeps `!fir.ref<T>`,
`!fir.ptr<T>` (POINTER), `!fir.heap<T>` (ALLOCATABLE) and
`!fir.llvm_ptr<T>` apart, and the pointer kinds "are explicitly
distinguished to disallow the composition of multiple levels of
indirection. For example, an ALLOCATABLE POINTER is invalid"
(`flang/include/flang/Optimizer/Dialect/FIRTypes.td:179-187, 266-299,
374-384`); legal nestings spell the Fortran attribute
(`fir.ref<fir.box<fir.heap<fir.array<?xf32>>>>` for `REAL, ALLOCATABLE ::
X(:)`, `flang/docs/HighLevelFIR.md:320`). The design doc states the rule:
"except for the POINTER and ALLOCATABLE attributes that are retrievable
from the SSA value type, other Fortran attributes (OPTIONAL, TARGET,
VOLATILE...) will not be retrievable from the SSA value alone"; passes
"will be allowed to look for" the declaring op "but not to assume that it
must be visible" (`HighLevelFIR.md:86-99`). Alias analysis reads the
pointer kind off the type (`flang/docs/AliasingAnalysisFIR.md:69-86`).

F2. *(read)* **Effects derived from a type parameter, changed by one
cast.** `!fir.ref<T, volatile>` has `isVolatile` as a parameter; every
memory op calls `addVolatileMemoryEffects(types, effects)`, adding Read
and Write on a non-addressable `VolatileMemoryResource` whenever an
operand type is volatile (`flang/include/flang/Optimizer/Dialect/
FIROpsSupport.h:20-39`, `FIRTypes.td:377-386`); `fir.convert` may not
change volatility, only `fir.volatile_cast` may (`FIROps.td:2734-2743`).
The type is the one source; the effect is computed from it.

F3. *(read)* **Values against variables.** HLFIR's `!hlfir.expr<...>` is
an "Abstract value type for Fortran arrays, characters and derived
types" (`flang/include/flang/Optimizer/HLFIR/HLFIROpBase.td:48-87`); "the
ALLOCATABLE, POINTER, TARGET, VOLATILE, ASYNCHRONOUS, OPTIONAL aspects do
not apply to expressions, they apply to variables" (`HighLevelFIR.md:
236-237`); a scalar with an SSA form is never wrapped (`:173-178`). The
rejected alternative, one box type for both, failed because "it would
then not really be possible to know if a fir.box is an expression or a
variable ... while a hlfir.expr value cannot change, which allows moving
its usages more easily" (`:1407-1428`). Ownership of a temporary is
threaded as an `i1` (`hlfir.associate` returns `(var, base, i1
must_free)`, `hlfir.end_associate %v, %must_free`, `hlfir.as_expr %v move
%must_free`), and `hlfir.destroy` marks a last use with a warning that
"transformations should not introduce any hlfir.expr usages after an
hlfir.destroy. The future will probably be to identify the last use
points automatically in bufferization instead" (`HLFIROps.td:870-996,
1222-1263`). Expression producers declare `Allocate` on their result, and
`end_associate`/`destroy` declare `Free` (`HLFIROps.cpp:36-68`,
`HLFIROps.td:872, 942, 1058-1064, 1222`).

F4. *(read)* **Stack and heap allocation: two ops, the result type and
the resource say which.** `fir.alloca` returns `Res<fir_ReferenceType,
"", [MemAlloc<AutomaticAllocationScopeResource>]>`; `fir.allocmem`
returns `Res<fir_HeapType, "", [MemAlloc<DefaultResource>]>` and "must be
paired with `freemem`" (`FIROps.td:84-297`).

F5. *(read)* **Arithmetic: one op per operation in ClangIR, signedness in
the type.** CIR has `cir.add`, `cir.sub`, `cir.mul`, `cir.div`,
`cir.rem`, ..., `cir.fadd`..., with `nsw`/`nuw`/saturated as `UnitProp`s
on a shared base class, and checked arithmetic as a separate two-result
op (`%r, %o = cir.add.overflow %a, %b : !cir.int<s, 33> -> !s32i`)
(`clang/include/clang/CIR/Dialect/IR/CIROps.td:2658-2715, 2750-3172`).
Signedness is in `!cir.int<s, 32>`, so `cir.div` is one op whose meaning
follows its type (`CIRTypes.td:38-56`, `CIROps.td:2898-2913`); POP does
the same with its dtype (G4). This is the opposite of B3, and right for a
dialect that models a source language whose types are signed. Comparisons
and casts take an enum (`cir.cmp gt`, `cir.cast array_to_ptrdecay`,
`CIROps.td:190-366, 2575-2652`). The separate ops are grouped by op
interfaces, `CIR_BinaryOpInterface` and `CIR_UnaryOpInterface`, attached
by the base classes (`clang/include/clang/CIR/Interfaces/
CIROpInterfaces.td:297-342`).

F6. *(read)* **Recursive records are mutable types, done carefully.**
`!cir.struct` and `!cir.union` declare `MutableType`; `mutate()` turns an
incomplete record complete and a second completion must agree "in every
parameter"; printing and parsing use `tryStartCyclicPrint` and
`tryStartCyclicParse` (`CIRTypes.td:863-923, 1038-1085`; `CIRTypesDetails.
h:68-116`; `CIRTypes.cpp:234-300`). Name-keyed identity has a cost: the
ABI pass renames records with a `__post_abi_` prefix and warns of a
storage conflict if the strip runs early (`CIRTypes.td:1001-1023`). FIR's
`!fir.type<name{...}>` mutates through `getImpl()` setters outside
MLIR's `mutate()` and guards recursive printing with a file-scope static
set (`flang/lib/Optimizer/Dialect/FIRType.cpp:157-195, 741-742`): the
cautionary form.

F7. *(read)* **A fake resource gives an op identity.** FIR's
non-addressable `DebuggingResource` keeps `hlfir.declare` alive under DCE
(`MemAlloc<DebuggingResource>`) and keeps two inlined `fir.dummy_scope`s
distinct under CSE (`MemWrite<DebuggingResource>`: they "must remain
distinct during the optimizations") (`flang/include/flang/Optimizer/
Dialect/FIROps.h:50-62`, `FIROps.td:3475-3553`, `HLFIROps.td:32-40`).

F8. *(read)* **Aliases readable by construction.** CIR's
`OpAsmDialectInterface::getAlias` prints `!s32i`, `!u8i`, `!rec_Node`,
`!void` (`clang/lib/CIR/Dialect/IR/CIRDialect.cpp:45-93`); casts print the
kind first (`cir.cast array_to_ptrdecay %0 : !cir.ptr<!cir.array<i32 x
10>> -> !cir.ptr<i32>`); loads print both types (`cir.load %0 :
!cir.ptr<i32>, i32`, `CIROps.td:879`); flags go in an order-free
`oilist(...)` (`:802-810`); FIR and HLFIR read as English (`fir.store %v
to %p`, `hlfir.assign %rhs to %lhs realloc`). Lowering patterns are
generated from ODS fields on `CIR_Op` (`hasLLVMLowering`, `llvmOp =
"UnreachableOp"`, `CIROps.td:86-122`).

**G. The rest of Mojo's IR** (KGEN, POP, HLCF, CO; read through a
helper; paths under `third_party/modular/`).

G1. *(read)* **Every compile-time slot in a type is a `TypedAttr`.**
`!kgen.simd<size, dtype>` (`Mojo/include/Mojo/KGENDialect/KGENTypes.td:
1116-1120`), `!kgen.pointer<T, addrspace>` (`:569-614`), `!pop.array<size,
T>` (`Mojo/include/Mojo/POPDialect/POPTypes.td:34-58`); even
`isMemoryOnly` on `!kgen.struct` is a `TypedAttr` that may be "an
i1-typed constraint proposition" folded per instantiation
(`KGENTypes.td:816-820`). Op constraints accept a still-unknown dtype
(`UnboundOr<...>`, `:1146-1204`), so one op definition serves both
parametric and concrete IR.

G2. *(read)* **Expressions canonical by construction, and the warning
that came with it.** `#kgen.param.expr<opcode, operands...>`: "expression
building force a deterministic canonical form ... `(x+1)*4` and
`(x*4)+4` canonicalize into the same pointer value"; the builder "may not
necessarily return a ParamOperatorAttr" (`KGENAttrs.td:2407-2454`). Its
author, on the opcode enum: "The list above ^^^ is massively out of
control, these things are not 'operators' like ParamOperatorAttr was
designed for. MLIR allows us to define new attributes, and it's really
good at it! Let's stop this pattern ... -ChrisL" (`KGENEnums.td:
109-115`). One generic attribute with an enum grew into a grab-bag; the
fix named is one attribute class per concept. Parameters are referred to
by de Bruijn index inside uniqued types, "useful for later knowing if two
types are equal, even if they have different parameter names"
(`KGENAttrInterfaces.td:134-171`).

G3. *(read)* **Nominal types break cycles through symbols; no mutable
types.** No KGEN, POP or LIT type is mutable or recursive: nominal
structs are `kgen.struct.generator`/`kgen.struct.instance` symbol ops
referred to from types by `#kgen.genref`/`#kgen.instref`
(`KGENOps.td:352-423`, `KGENAttrs.td:1826-1880`); the anonymous
`!kgen.struct<(...)>` keeps only what layout needs (`KGENTypes.td:
774-779`). `!kgen.variant<...>` is "a structural variant type ...
(discriminated union)" that keeps its discriminant because "Before
elaboration, the compiler doesn't have a target model"
(`KGENTypes.td:985-1027`, `POPOps.td:2472-2478`).

G4. *(read)* **POP: one op per operation, an enum only for comparison;
signedness from the dtype; purity per op.** `pop.add/sub/mul/div/rem/
floordiv/max/min/shl/shr`, one `pop.cmp lt(%a, %b)` with
`eq ne lt gt le ge` and no signed/unsigned split; "The signedness of
integer division is determined by the dtype"; `add/sub/mul` are `Pure`,
`div/rem/floordiv` `NoMemoryEffect` only, since division by zero is
undefined (`POPOps.td:70-569`, `KGENEnums.td:122-135`).

G5. *(read)* **Coarse effects, no resources, no tokens.** No
`SideEffects::Resource` is defined anywhere in KGEN, POP, HLCF, CO or M;
effects go to the default resource through `Res<..., [MemAlloc]>`,
`Arg<..., [MemFree|MemRead|MemWrite]>` or C++ `getEffects` where a flag
is parametric; calls (`kgen.call*`, `pop.external_call`) declare nothing
and are unknown. Function-level effects are the `FnEffects` bits in the
function type (A4). HLCF uses its own control-flow interfaces instead of
`RegionBranchOpInterface` and reimplements canonicalizer, SCCP, mem2reg
and SROA (`Mojo/include/Mojo/HLCFDialect/HLCFInterfaces.td:19-96`;
`Mojo/include/Mojo/KGENPasses.td:32-39, 711-1188`). Modular's own TODOs
record duplicated representations (`KGENAttrs.td:1835, 2831`; the
generator's signature held twice, `KGENOps.td:189-239`) and attributes
that mirror ops (`POPAttrs.td:746-750`).

G6. *(read)* **What Mojo erases and when.** `check-lifetimes` "inserts
destructor calls after the last use of a value ... and implements a borrow
checker" (`KGENPasses.td:123-129`); after it, `!lit.ref` becomes
`!kgen.pointer` (A2), origins are gone ("cannot be used in things like
'comptime if' that are evaluated at elaboration time (because origins are
gone by then)", `LITAttrs.td:519-523`), and `lower-arg-conventions`
lowers conventions (`KGENPasses.td:675-705`). Ownership lives in types and
function types up to one checking pass, then is erased in one step.

**E. CIRCT** (read through a helper from a shallow clone of
github.com/llvm/circt at c9141501, 2026-10-20; paths relative to it; the
in-tree `docs/` are circt.llvm.org's source).

E1. *(read)* **Qualifiers as plain parameters on one base class, and
recursive properties cached at construction.** Every FIRRTL hardware type
stores `isConst`, width types `widthOrSentinel` (-1 = not yet inferred),
printed `!firrtl.uint<8>`, `!firrtl.uint`, `!firrtl.const.uint<4>`
(`include/circt/Dialect/FIRRTL/FIRRTLTypesImpl.td:43-127`,
`lib/Dialect/FIRRTL/FIRRTLTypes.cpp:46-65`). The history: "Having the
known width and unknown width types implemented with two different C++
classes was awkward, led to casting bugs, and prevented having a
`FIRRTLType` class that unified all the FIRRTL dialect types"
(`docs/Dialects/FIRRTL/RationaleFIRRTL.md:444-451`). Each aggregate's
storage computes `RecursiveTypeProperties {isPassive, containsReference,
containsAnalog, containsConst, containsTypeAlias, hasUninferredWidth,
hasUninferredReset}` once (`FIRRTLTypes.h:73-88`,
`FIRRTLTypes.cpp:1464-1510`), so `isRegisterType()` is three bit tests
(`FIRRTLTypes.h:197-200`) and ODS constraints are written over the same
bits (`PassiveType`, `SizedType`, `FIRRTLTypes.td:88-116`).

E2. *(read)* **What is not a type.** "Flow is _not_ represented as a
first-class type in CIRCT. We instead provide utilities for computing flow
when needed"; an outer flip on ports "was an inelegant abuse of an
unrelated concept that added bloat to the type system. Many operations
would have to check for an outer flip on ports and immediately discard
it" (`RationaleFIRRTL.md:588-590, 648-667`). An earlier flip
canonicalization was removed because "this resulted in information loss
during canonicalization ... Type canonicalization may make illegal
operations legal" (`:472-482`). Normalizing a type is right only when no
op's legality depends on the difference.

E3. *(read)* **Parameters are attributes because types cannot hold SSA
values; builders return the simpler type.** "because MLIR types are
immortal and uniqued, they can refer to attributes but cannot refer to SSA
values ... Operations need to be able to compute their own type without
creating other operations" (`docs/Dialects/HW/RationaleHW.md:456-463`);
`!hw.int<width>`'s builder "may return a builtin integer type if the
width is a known-constant value" (`include/circt/Dialect/HW/
HWTypesImpl.td:30-51`). `!hw.typealias<@scope::@name, inner>` stores the
canonical inner type next to the name because "the parser cannot look up
the declaration when it builds the type" (`RationaleHW.md:512-552`).

E4. *(read)* **Sign on the op where the computation differs; one op with
an enum for comparisons; no redundant ops.** "`comb` operates on signless
types with signless operations ... division, have different behaviors for
signed v.s. unsigned types, thus they are modeled with different ops
(`divu` and `divs`)" (`docs/Dialects/Comb/RationaleComb.md:51-59`); the
mnemonics are exactly ours: `divs`, `divu`, `mods`, `modu`, `shrs`,
`shru` (`include/circt/Dialect/Comb/Combinational.td:76-87`); `comb.icmp`
takes a predicate (`:103-147`). "No `~x` complement or `-x` negation
operator: instead use `comb.xor(x, -1)` or `comb.sub(0, x)` ... No zero
extension operator ... strictly redundant with `concat(zero, value)`";
the goal is "to make the IR more regular, easy to transform, and have
fewer canonical forms"; a multibit mux was refused because "It is another
operation that many transformations would need to be aware of"
(`RationaleComb.md:92-166`).

E5. *(read)* **One op with optional operands, or two ops: CIRCT does
both, and says why.** FIRRTL has `firrtl.reg` and `firrtl.regreset`
(`FIRRTLDeclarations.td:384-400, 493-510`), and canonicalization moves
between them (`regreset(clock, constant_zero, v) -> reg(clock)`,
`FIRRTLCanonicalization.td:669-672`). `seq.compreg` takes
`Optional<I1>:$reset, Optional<AnyType>:$resetValue` because "This operand
makes lowering to an efficient implementation of reset easier" and
"Providing support for all flavors of registers is an anti-goal of this
op" (`include/circt/Dialect/Seq/SeqOps.td:33-60`;
`docs/Dialects/Seq/RationaleSeq.md:81-92`). *(conjecture, from these
cases)* The split follows what the consumers of the op share, not a rule
about optional operands.

E6. *(read)* **State without memory effects: observability is an
inherent attribute.** Registers are `Pure` SSA ops in graph regions
(`SeqOps.td:33-34, 231-234`); a value whose producer must survive carries
an `inner_sym`: "When the symbol is defined, the entity is considered part
of the visible interface and should be preserved in transformation"
(`RationaleHW.md:563-566`). Where CIRCT does use effects, it uses them to
stop CSE: `firrtl.invalidvalue` is `MemAlloc` because "Each invalid op
produces a unique invalid value" (`FIRRTLExpressions.td:233-245`).
`DropConst` "drops the 'const' modifier from all types ... so that they do
not need to take 'const' into account" (`include/circt/Dialect/FIRRTL/
Passes.td:728-736`): a phase type removed when its checks are done.

E7. *(read)* **Syntax.** `hw.module @test1(in %arg0: i3, in %arg1: i1,
out result: i50)` (`test/Dialect/HW/basic.mlir:4`); a required name
attribute is printed only when it differs from the result's SSA name
(`include/circt/Support/CustomDirectiveImpl.h:51-57`); one type when
operands and result agree (`comb.add %a, %b : i12`); `UnitAttr:$twoState`
prints as `bin`; indices stored, names printed (`hw.struct_inject` stores
`fieldIndex`, prints `%input["field"]`, `HWAggregates.td:283-291`).

**H. Discourse, talks, and a linear-value dialect.**

H1. *(read)* **"No implicit semantics in op names."** The Linalg "Op
explosion" RFC (discourse.llvm.org/t/rfc-op-explosion-in-linalg/82863,
rengolin, 2024): with about 30 convolution ops and many matmul variants,
"Every pattern becomes a large `switch/case` statement"; casts,
broadcasts, transposes and quantization should be expressed through affine
maps or attributes; ops "stay separate only where code can't be shared";
the elementwise family became one op with a kind attribute (B12). This is
§4's polymorphic ladder, met and named upstream.

H2. *(read)* **Dialect design talk.** "MLIR Dialect Design and
Composition for Front-End Compilers" (Jeff Niu, Modular, EuroLLVM 2023,
llvm.org/devmtg/2023-05, video youtu.be/hIt6J1_E21c). Its abstract opens
"MLIR dialect design is often more an art than a science" and names
preserving high-level information and separating "structural" from
"computation" dialects as the techniques, from building Mojo. (The page
lists slides but links none; the video was not watched.)

H3. *(read)* **A linear-value dialect upstream of nobody: CUDA-Q's
Quake.** Quake keeps two forms of the same object in one dialect: a memory
form, where "quantum operators act as side-effects on qubit references"
(`!quake.ref`), and a value form, where "operators consume values and
return new values" (`%q0_1 = quake.op %q0_0 : (!quake.wire) ->
!quake.wire`); "These values are not truly SSA values ... the value cannot
be copied"; `quake.unwrap` takes the current wire out of a reference and
`quake.wrap` writes it back; "Reference and value forms can coexist within
the same function body"; transformations "must preserve this threading"
(nvidia.github.io/cuda-quantum/latest/specification/quake-dialect.html,
"Motivation", "Calling between reference and value forms"). The page
names no verifier for the no-copy rule.

#### 5.8.2 What it means for idris-mlir: a design guide

Every rule below is grounded in 5.8.1; every sentence about our dialect
is *conjecture* until wave 2 reads `foreign/idr`, since this lane worked
from §2 alone.

**The lens.** Read through the representation-first document, the
eleven sources above say one thing: a dialect's complexity is decided by
*where each fact lives*, and a ladder in C++ is a fact living in the
wrong carrier. MLIR offers five carriers, and the question that picks
one is *who needs the fact*:

| who needs the fact | carrier | exemplars |
| --- | --- | --- |
| every use of the value, every pass | a type, or an attribute inside a type | FIR `ref`/`ptr`/`heap` (F1); `!lit.ref` origins (A2); `!ptr.ptr<#space>` (B6) |
| one op's meaning | an inherent attribute or property of that op | `arith.cmpi`'s predicate (B4); `linalg.elementwise <kind>` (B12) |
| what an op may do to state | a declared effect on a resource | `AllocLikeOp<mnemonic, Resource>` (C3); handle consumption (C4) |
| a classification over many ops or types | a trait or an interface | `ValueSemantics`, `TensorLikeType` (B7); `CIR_BinaryOpInterface` (F5) |
| a property of a whole function | an analysis, or an invariant checked by a pass | CIRCT's flow (E2); `transform-dialect-check-uses` (B10) |

A fact in a lower row than its readers need is rederived (the
rederivation chains of §1); a fact in a higher row than needed costs casts
(B3's "cast from sbyte to ubyte", LLVM's typed pointers, `docs/Dialects/
LLVM.md:263-266`: opaque pointers move "behavior relevant to the pointee
type into operations rather than into types").

##### Naming

**N1. A mnemonic is `family.verb` (or `family.noun` for a constructor),
and the family names the type or resource acted on.** `lit.ref.load`,
`lit.ref.store`, `kgen.struct.extract`, `kgen.variant.create` (A5, G),
`memref.alloca_scope`, `hw.module.extern`, `seq.compreg.ce` (E), CIR's
`vec.*` and `complex.*` (F8). Leaves are snake case (`index_switch`,
`get_member`). A dot opens a family; it never encodes a variant that a
type or an attribute already distinguishes.
*For §2 (conjecture):* the prefixes are partial today ("some under
`str.`", "some under `array.`", `big.` "on most"); make them total, every
op in exactly one family named after its type (`data.`, `cell.`, `big.`,
`str.`, `array.`, `lazy.`) or its resource (`io.`, `rc.` or `own.`).

**N2. No implicit semantics in names** (H1). A name does not repeat what
an operand type says, and a family whose members differ only in the pure
function they compute is one op with an enum (B4). Members whose
computations differ stay separate ops (B3), defined by one TableGen
class and grouped by one op interface, as arith's `Arith_IntBinaryOp`
classes (`ArithOps.td:33-216`) and CIR's `CIR_BinaryOp` with
`CIR_BinaryOpInterface` (F5) are.
*For §2:* `put_sint`, `put_uint`, `put_double`, `show (sint, uint,
double)`, `int_head_s`, `int_head_u`, `double_head`, the six stat-time ops
and the eight clock ops are the candidates (5.8.3, DS4).

##### Types

**T1. In MLIR the type is the only per-value carrier every
transformation keeps.** A value has no attributes of its own: a
function's per-argument attributes are dictionaries held by the function
op (`OptionalAttr<DictArrayAttr>:$arg_attrs`, `include/mlir/Dialect/
Func/IR/FuncOps.td:55-56`), whose entries are dialect attributes
(`docs/LangRef.md:787-827`), and a block argument of any other region
has none; RAUW and region inlining keep a value's type (*recalled*).
SIL keeps ownership "on the value" and checks it with its own verifier
(D1); in MLIR, "on the value" can only mean "in the value's type". This
is AGENTS.md's rule seen from MLIR's side: grades are types because
nothing else survives every pass. The price is B1: strict type equality,
so every regrade is an op and every op that accepts several grades says so
in its operand constraint.

**T2. A qualifier is an attribute parameter implementing an interface,
and ops ask it.** `!ptr.ptr<#space>` with `MemorySpaceAttrInterface`'s
`isValidLoad`/`isValidStore` (B6), memref's layout and tensor's encoding
(B6), Mojo's origins whose type `!lit.origin<isMutable>` carries the
mutability (A2). The grade's algebra (may it be duplicated, discarded,
written in place; the join of two grades at a merge; which regrades are
legal) is written once, in the grade attribute, and every verifier and
lowering asks it.
*For §2:* the three aliases `!idr.lin`, `!idr.own`, `!idr.excl` already
point at a small closed set of grades; the interface makes the ops that
treat grades uniformly one op each (5.8.3, DS1).

**T3. One meaning, one uniqued type: normalize at construction.** The
memref identity layout is "discarded on construction" (B5), CIR's default
address space is normalized to the empty attribute (F, `CIRTypes.td:
252-310`), `!hw.int<32>` returns the builtin `i32` (E3), Mojo's origin
builders "fold the request automatically" (A3). The unrestricted grade is
the bare type, never a wrapper. CIRCT's limit applies (E2): normalize only
where no op's legality depends on the difference.

**T4. Make illegal grade combinations unrepresentable.** Two independent
parameters (quantity, permission) admit their product; if only some
pairs are meaningful (the brief names three plus the unwrapped type),
the product is Insight 4's three booleans. One enum of the legal grades
replaces it. FIR does the same with its pointer grammar, refusing
`heap<ptr<..>>` in the type verifier (F1). Erased quantity 0 is already
unrepresentable as a value (AGENTS.md); the type's quantity has only two
values left.

**T5. Lifetimes are regions first, attributes second, never SSA values.**
MLIR forbids SSA values in types (B2, E3). Its native lifetime is a
region: `AutomaticAllocationScope` ("allocations are automatically freed
when control is transferred back from the regions of such operations",
`docs/Traits/_index.md:225-235`) and `memref.alloca_scope`
(`MemRefOps.td:424-454`). A borrow whose scope is a region needs no
lifetime in its type: a local verifier on the region's terminator stops
it escaping, where SIL needs begin/end pairs checked by joint
post-dominance (D4), a non-local check MLIR's verifier doctrine avoids
(B10). Where a lifetime must be named in a type (a result that borrows
from an argument), Mojo's form is the model: a symbolic attribute that
normalizes on construction (A2, A3) and is erased in one step after the
one pass that checks it (G6).
*For §2:* the fourth parameter of `!idr.q` is the place to look.

**T6. A function type carries what a caller must know: conventions and
effects.** `!kgen.func`'s metadata holds per-argument `ArgConvention`s
and the `FnEffects` bits, uniqued with the type (A4); SIL's lowered
function type carries `@owned`, `@guaranteed`, `@inout` (D4); Koka's
effect rows are the same idea for a pure language
(`sources/papers/leijen-2017-koka-effects`, *recalled*). A call's effects
are then read off the callee's type, once, instead of being re-inferred
(§2's `Analysis/Effects`). The catch is B9: a direct call's callee is a
sibling symbol, which a parallel function pass may not read; 5.8.3, DS2
weighs the options.

**T7. Classify types with traits and interfaces, and cache recursive
properties in the type's storage.** `ValueSemantics`, `TensorLikeType`,
`BufferLikeType`, the transform handle interfaces (B7); FIRRTL's
`RecursiveTypeProperties`, computed once per type so a classification is
a bit test (E1). Implement upstream's interfaces where the meaning
matches (`DataLayoutTypeInterface`; `MemRefElementTypeInterface` and
`VectorElementTypeInterface` for word-like types).
*For §2:* `ValueFacts` is the natural home of "contains a linear field",
"contains a reference", "is trivially copyable", "has a runtime form",
computed at construction, not per query.

**T8. Recursive types are the one place a type may need a mutable or
external body, and each choice has a cost.** LLVM's and CIR's identified
structs mutate once under MLIR's `mutate` (B8, F6); Mojo breaks cycles
through symbols and has no mutable type (G3), which a parallel function
pass cannot read if the declaration is a sibling (B9); an attribute on an
ancestor op is readable (B9). 5.8.3, DS3 ranks them.

##### Op granularity

**O1. One op per computation; one op with an enum when the variants share
operand and result structure, traits, effects and verification.** The
line upstream draws (B3, B4), the same line comb (E4), CIR (F5) and POP
(G4) draw, and the one the Linalg RFC restated: separate ops "only where
code can't be shared" (H1). An enum that changes an op's effects, its
undefined behaviour or its valid states is a flag (the representation-
first skill's "One op per semantics"); an enum that selects the pure
function in a shared shape is not.

**O2. Two ops that differ only in their effect are one TableGen class
parameterized by the resource.** `memref.alloc` and `memref.alloca` are
`AllocLikeOp<mnemonic, Resource>` (C3); `fir.alloca` and `fir.allocmem`
differ in result type and resource (F4). An op whose effects are those of
its region is one op with `RecursiveMemoryEffects` (`scf.index_switch`,
`SCFOps.td:1172`; `memref.alloca_scope`, `MemRefOps.td:424-430`), never a
pure op plus an IO twin. Sketch for our cells (*conjecture*):

```tablegen
class Idr_CellAllocOp<string mnemonic, Resource where>
    : Idr_Op<mnemonic, [DeclareOpInterfaceMethods<OpAsmOpInterface,
                                                  ["getAsmResultNames"]>]> {
  let arguments = (ins Variadic<AnyType>:$fields);
  let results = (outs Res<Idr_CellType, "", [MemAlloc<where, 0, FullEffect>]>:$cell);
}
def Idr_CellNewOp   : Idr_CellAllocOp<"cell.new",   Idr_HeapResource>;
def Idr_CellStackOp : Idr_CellAllocOp<"cell.stack", AutomaticAllocationScopeResource>;
```

**O3. A regrade is a cast whose result type is computed and whose
legality is the grade's; an ownership event with a runtime cost is its
own op with its own effect.** `lit.ref.immut` computes its result type
with `TypesMatchWith ... getWithMutability(false)` (A5);
`fir.volatile_cast` is the only op that may change volatility (F2). SIL
keeps `copy_value` and `destroy_value` (counting) apart from
`begin_borrow` and `move_value` (no count) (D3). Forwarding is a category
(D2): an op that carries an operand into a result declares which, once,
in an interface; projections forward only non-consuming grades; consuming
an aggregate is one destructuring op.

**O4. A lower form is another op, often of another dialect, never a flag
on the high one.** SIL's `strong_retain` is "_not_ available in OSSA"
(D3); Linalg's forms are distinct ops of one meaning (B12); Mojo lowers
`!lit.ref` to `!kgen.pointer` and LIT to KGEN to POP (A2, G6). Upstream
dialects take what they already mean (the skill's "Upstream dialects
before ours"); our ops that exist only before or only after a pass are
legal or illegal through `ConversionTarget`, not through attributes.

**O5. Compose before fusing.** An op that computes a pure value and then
performs an effect is two ops in the canonical form, the effect living in
one op family (*conjecture*, from O1 and C1): `io.put_sint %x` is
`io.write (str.show_sint %x)`, fused at lowering when the runtime has a
faster entry point. The pure half then folds and CSEs with upstream's
drivers.

##### Effects

*(The effects rules are numbered EF to keep them apart from CIRCT's
findings E1 to E7.)*

**EF1. Two interfaces for four behaviours.** Memory effects on resources
for state (IO, heap cells, references, memo state);
`ConditionallySpeculatable` for undefined behaviour and loops (C1). An
abort that must be kept is an effect, as `cf.assert` writes (C6);
undefined behaviour is `ub.unreachable`, a terminator with no effect.
Totality is a trait on the op, as `Arith_TotalIntBinaryOp` adds `Pure`
(C2). Whether non-termination is kept (an effect) or only blocks
speculation is a semantic question for the owner (5.8.4).

**EF2. Resources form a tree; design ours as one.** Resources have parents
and disjointness, and CSE reads disjointness (§5.2's A3;
`SideEffectsAndSpeculation.md:78-96`). A sketch (*conjecture*): a
non-addressable `World` with children `Console`, `Files`, `Environment`,
`Clock`, `Process`; a crash writes `World`, so it stays after every
observable effect; reading arguments or the clock is then disjoint from
writing the console, and CSE and reordering see it.

**EF3. Consumption is an effect on the value.** The transform dialect's
`Read` + `Free` on a consumed operand and `Allocate` + `Write` on a
produced result (C4) are SIL's lifetime-ending bit (D1) said in MLIR's
own vocabulary: one ODS declaration, `Arg<T, "", [MemRead<Idr_Linear>,
MemFree<Idr_Linear>]>`, read by DCE (a consumer is never trivially dead),
by CSE (two consumers never merge) and by the linearity check. A
whole-function invariant such as "consumed exactly once on every path" is
a pass, run after every pass by instrumentation if AGENTS.md's "verified
after every pass" needs it, not an op verifier (B10).
*For §2:* `ConsumesOperands` and `ConsumesOnly` would become these
declarations, and the `Lin` resource is already the right shape if it is
used this way.

**EF4. Tokens order asynchrony; effects order state.** GPU tokens order
device work and the same ops keep their memory effects (C5); neither
Mojo (G5) nor CIRCT (E6) threads a token through effectful code; the
builtin `token` cannot cross a call, a branch, a loop or a region result
(B11). A world value kept beside declared effects is a second
representation of "does IO" (§2's three); if it stays, its effects must be
*derived* from it by one rule, the way FIR derives volatile effects from
the operand type in one helper (F2). 5.8.3, DS2 ranks the options.

**EF5. A fake resource only where the representation cannot say it.**
FIR's `DebuggingResource` keeps declarations alive and inlined scopes
distinct (F7); CIRCT's `invalidvalue` uses `MemAlloc` to stop CSE (E6).
These are effects standing in for identity; prefer a type or an
operand.

##### Syntax

**S1. Every op has a declarative assembly format; print each type once.**
`comb.add %a, %b : i12` and `arith.addi %a, %b : i64` print one type when
operands and results agree (E7; `ArithOps.td:52-56`); `cir.load %0 :
!cir.ptr<i32>, i32` prints both when they differ (F8); enum keywords come
first (`arith.cmpi slt, ...`, `cir.cast array_to_ptrdecay ...`,
`pop.cmp lt(%a, %b)`); English connectives read well (`fir.store %v to
%p`, `hlfir.assign %rhs to %lhs`); any remaining flags go in an order-free
`oilist` (`cir.alloca`, `CIROps.td:802-810`).

**S2. Readable aliases, generated.** CIR's `OpAsmDialectInterface::
getAlias` yields `!s32i`, `!rec_Node` (F8); `genMnemonicAlias` gives a
type its mnemonic as alias (`AttributesAndTypes.md:1216-1222`). A data
type prints as `!List_Nat`, a grade as `!lin_List_Nat` or similar, once
per module.

**S3. Result names, stored indices, printed names.** `getAsmResultNames`
(`memref.alloc`'s, `MemRefOps.td:227-228`); CIRCT prints a name only when
it differs from the SSA name (E7) and prints a field's name while storing
its index (`hw.struct_inject`, E7). A constructor or a field is an index
into its data type, printed by name.

#### 5.8.3 Candidate designs, ranked

*(Numbered DS to keep them apart from SIL's findings D1 to D5.)*

**DS1. The graded type.** The brief: `!idr.q<quantity, permission, T,
lifetime>`, with `!idr.lin<T>` = (one, plain), `!idr.own<T>` = (many,
own), `!idr.excl<T>` = (many, excl).

1. *One grade attribute with an interface, normalized, illegal pairs
   unrepresentable* (T2, T3, T4). A type `!idr.g<T, #idr.grade>` whose
   grade is one of the legal grades (an enum attribute implementing an
   `Idr_GradeAttrInterface`: `mayDuplicate`, `mayDiscard`, `isUnique`,
   `join`, `canRegradeTo`), whose builder returns `T` itself for the
   unrestricted grade, and whose pretty forms are the aliases we already
   print:

   ```mlir
   // exemplars:  !ptr.ptr<#ptr.generic_space>   memref<?xf32> (identity layout dropped)
   //             !lit.ref<f32, mut some_origin>
   %xs = idr.call @build() : () -> !idr.lin<!List_Nat>   // !idr.g<!List_Nat, #idr.lin>
   %ys = idr.own.share %xs : !idr.lin<!List_Nat> -> !idr.own<!List_Nat>
   %n  = idr.call @length(%ys) : (!idr.own<!List_Nat>) -> i64   // i64: no wrapper
   ```

   Buys: the grade algebra in one place; ops verified by asking it; the
   ownership ops that today differ by grade become one op each (DS4).
   Costs: none beyond today's (every grade boundary is already an op, B1);
   a migration of every grade-reading `switch`. Deletes: per-grade ops and
   C++ that switches on (quantity, permission). The lifetime parameter
   leaves the type if borrows become regions (T5), or stays as a
   normalized symbolic attribute (Mojo's form) if wave 2 shows a result
   must name one.
2. *One type per grade* (FIR's `ref`/`ptr`/`heap`, F1): `!idr.lin<T>`,
   `!idr.own<T>`, `!idr.excl<T>` as three TypeDefs. Buys: ODS constraints
   by type name. Costs: the algebra spread over three classes; CIRCT
   found two C++ classes for one concept "awkward, led to casting bugs"
   (E1).
3. *Keep `!idr.q`'s four parameters.* Costs: the product of states
   (T4), and every reader re-deciding which pairs are legal.
4. *SIL's form: ownership on the defining op or block argument, not the
   type.* Rejected by T1 and AGENTS.md: such facts live in discardable or
   per-op places a pass can drop.

**DS2. The world and IO effects.**

1. *No world value after the frontend: IO ops declare effects on a
   resource tree (EF2), and functions carry their effects in their type
   (T6).* The idiom of memref, Mojo and CIRCT (EF4). `unsafePerformIO` (a
   trusted library's forged world, AGENTS.md) becomes a region op with
   `RecursiveMemoryEffects`, whose effects stay visible where the value is
   demanded. For direct calls, B9 forbids reading the callee's type from
   a parallel function pass, so either the call carries the callee's
   effect set as an inherent property, verified against the callee by
   `SymbolUserOpInterface::verifySymbolUses` (a cache with one source,
   checked; CIR's `cir.call` keeps an inherent `memory_effects`, F,
   `CIROps.td:4676-4686`), or the effects are an analysis cached in the
   `AnalysisManager` and read by our passes (upstream's CSE and DCE then
   see a call as unknown). Deletes: the world type, `world.new`, the
   world operands and results threaded through every IO op and call, and
   two of the three representations of "does IO".
2. *Keep the world as a value, derive every IO effect from it by one
   rule* (F2's pattern: an op with a world operand writes `World`). Buys:
   ordering visible in SSA, which a reader can follow. Costs: the world
   threaded through every loop and region, and still a second fact beside
   the effect unless the derivation is generated.
3. *The builtin `token`.* Rejected: it cannot be forwarded through calls,
   branches or region results (B11).

**DS3. Recursive data types** (the brief: "`data` is a mutable type: a
name whose body the program's table holds once"; §3's 98c6315c and
73bba53f were walls of this design).

1. *An immutable table on the ancestor `program` op, built once, and
   types that name an entry by index* (a coordinate, not a lookup by
   string). Readable from any nested pass (B9); no mutation, so no
   context lock on reads; printed by name through an alias (S2, S3).
   Costs: every structural query goes through the ancestor's attribute;
   adding a type rebuilds the table (73bba53f's cost) unless the frontend
   emits it whole.
2. *Identified types mutated once, CIR's way* (`MutableType`,
   `mutate()` with an agreement check, cyclic print and parse guards,
   F6). The upstream-sanctioned form (B8). Costs: name-keyed identity
   (CIR's `__post_abi_` rename); the mutable component is outside
   uniquing.
3. *Structural recursive types with de Bruijn back-references*
   (`!idr.data<"List", [Nil, Cons(!idr.nat, !idr.rec<0>)]>`): immutable,
   uniqued, readable anywhere, no table. KGEN's de Bruijn parameter
   references inside uniqued types are the precedent (G2). Costs: a
   canonical form for mutual recursion; no upstream dialect does it;
   unmeasured at the stage-1 module's type count.
4. *Symbol-reference types with declaration ops* (Mojo, G3). Rejected
   for us by B9: the declarations are siblings of the functions that read
   them.

**DS4. A first mapping of §2's surface** (*conjecture* throughout; each

#### 5.8.4 Questions for the owner, and for wave 2

**For the owner.**
- **DD-Q1. Grades on trivially copyable types.** Swift erases ownership
  wrappers on trivial types (D4) and MLIR's own normalizations drop
  defaults (B5). May `!idr.lin<i64>` normalize to `i64` at construction,
  or is that "dropping what Idris proved" (AGENTS.md)?
- **DD-Q2. Divergence.** May the compiler delete an unused call to a
  non-total function (upstream's model: non-termination blocks
  speculation only, C1), or is divergence an effect to keep?
- **DD-Q3. The world.** Effects only after the frontend (DS2.1), or a world
  value with derived effects (DS2.2)?
- **DD-Q4. Signedness.** arith's and comb's school (signless types, sign on
  the op where the computation differs, B3, E4), or CIR's and POP's
  (signed types, one op, F5, G4)? This lane recommends arith's: we lower
  to it, and the frontend has already monomorphized the source types.
- **DD-Q5. One op, several runtime functions.** May one op with an enum
  (`io.clock <kind>`, `idr.check <reason>`) map to several runtime
  functions, selected by a table generated from ODS, where
  `Idr_Runtime<"symbol">` ties one op to one function today?
- **DD-Q6. Borrows as regions** (T5), with no lifetime in the type, or
  lifetimes as symbolic attributes in types (Mojo)?
- **DD-Q7. Our own function op.** Should conventions and effects live in
  a custom function type (T6), which means keeping `idr.func`, or should
  we take `func.func` and keep them in an analysis?
- **DD-Q8. Renames.** Is every name open, including `con`, `dup`,
  `drop` and the `idr` namespace itself?

**For wave 2** (`ods`, `frontend-mirror` and `solvers` lanes).
- Which (quantity, permission) pairs occur, what the lifetime parameter
  holds, and who reads each (DS1, T4, T5).
- Which word ops differ from `arith` on inputs the runtime defines
  (division by zero, shift amounts at or past the width, rounding of
  `div` and `mod`), and why `shls` and `shlu` are two ops.
- For every pair or family flagged in DS4: its semantics, effects,
  verifier, folders, and every `isa<>` that tells its members apart.
- What `!idr.token` is, and whether the builtin `token` fits (B11).
- How the grade verifier runs (op verifiers or a pass), whether it
  follows def-use chains (B10), and its cost on the stage-1 module.
- How `data`'s mutability is used: when bodies are set, who reads them,
  and the lock path behind 98c6315c (DS3).
- Where `MayCrash`, `PerformsIO`, `ObservableIO`, `ConsumesOperands` and
  the world operands are read, mapped to the effect queries that would
  replace them (EF2, EF3).
- Every op with a `UnitAttr`, an optional operand or an enum, and whether
  it changes effects, undefined behaviour or valid states (O1).

#### 5.8.5 Notes for other lanes

- **mlir-ods (5.2):** the recipe table should carry four patterns this
  lane relies on: a TableGen op class parameterized by a `Resource`
  (C3); a qualifier attribute with an interface that verifiers ask (B6);
  `Arg<..., [MemFree<R>]>` as the lifetime-ending bit (C4, EF3);
  `TypesMatchWith` for casts whose result type is computed (A5). Also
  `genMnemonicAlias` and `OpAsmDialectInterface::getAlias` (S2).
- **mlir-analysis (5.1):** effects on values make consumers non-pure:
  check what that costs CSE and DCE on the stage-1 module. A direct call's
  effects read from the callee's type hit B9's sibling rule; how
  upstream's CSE reads a call's effects at 70,000 functions matters for
  DS2.1. `scf.index_switch` has a region per case as our `match` does
  (§3's e96bb991).
- **rc (5.10):** SIL's forwarding and "no partial consumption" rules (D2),
  `begin_cow_mutation`'s fresh SSA value (D4), ownership-based
  deallocation's `i1` ownership and per-function ABI (C7), HLFIR's
  `must_free` and its CSE warning on `hlfir.destroy` (F3), and Quake's
  two forms of one object (H3) are designs of ownership in an IR to set
  beside Perceus.
- **modular (5.4):** Mojo defines no effect resources, threads no tokens,
  leaves calls' effects unknown, and reimplements canonicalizer, SCCP,
  mem2reg and SROA (G5); its elaborator has a `max-depth` count option
  (`KGENPasses.td:500-502`), the kind of count AGENTS.md forbids as a
  reason to refuse. The `third_party/modular` `.td` files are the
  source of all of it.
- **mlton (5.9):** whether MLton's IRs keep representation choices in
  types (as DS1 and DS3 propose) or in side tables bears directly on DS3.
- **llvm (5.3):** TableGen class parameters over records (`Resource`),
  `!listconcat` trait lists (`MemRefOps.td:65-72`) and `ConcatI32Enum
  AtrCaseList` (`LinalgEnums.td:93-115`) are the generation idioms DS4
  leans on.
