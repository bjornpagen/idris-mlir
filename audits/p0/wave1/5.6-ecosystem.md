# 5.6 ecosystem: other large MLIR compilers, and RC compilers

- CIRCT: its whole-design analyses (`IMConstProp`, `IMDeadCodeElim`, the
  instance graph), whether and why it left the dataflow framework,
  threading over modules, its ODS and C++ conventions.
- IREE: its DFX solver framework (an Attributor for MLIR), its
  whole-program analyses and IPO, the stream dialect's resource
  analyses, its scale and threading.
- In the pinned tree: Flang's FIR analyses and alias analysis; ClangIR
  (`clang/lib/CIR`), its lifetime checking and effects.
- Others where they bear on it: Triton, torch-mlir, StableHLO, Enzyme's
  MLIR activity analysis (on the dataflow framework), Polygeist.
- Reference counting (Lean 4, Koka) is §5.10's and whole-program
  compilers outside MLIR (MLton) are §5.9's; this lane is MLIR codebases.

*Written (wave 1, 2026-10-21). Trees: CIRCT cloned at `c9141501`
(2026-10-20), IREE at `e7490c3b` (2026-10-21), both `--depth 1` under
`/tmp`; paths into them are relative to their roots and prefixed
`circt:` or `iree:`. Flang and ClangIR are read at the pin,
`.toolchain/llvm-project` at 7208ba24 (prefixed `llvm:`); single files
of the ClangIR incubator, Enzyme, Triton and torch-mlir were fetched at
the revisions named where they are cited.*

**In short.** None of the large MLIR codebases read here (CIRCT, IREE,
Flang, ClangIR, Triton) runs a whole-program, per-value solve on
upstream's `DataFlowSolver`; the two that do (torch-mlir's
`InlineGlobalSlots`, Enzyme's older activity analysis) run on small
modules, and Enzyme has since moved to per-function summaries (G1,
G3). CIRCT's whole-design analyses are
hand-written sparse solvers whose state exists only for what each
problem is about (A1-A6), and the one analysis CIRCT moved off the
framework ran 650 times faster for the reason our ffa91113 found (B5).
IREE's whole-program analyses are DFX, the Attributor ported to MLIR,
whose state is lazy, type-filtered and arena-allocated (C2), but whose
round budget IREE turns into compile errors (C3) and which it rebuilds
in every pass that needs it (D4). Everyone who keeps upstream's
framework scopes it to one function (D3, E1, G1, G2); nobody threads a
fixpoint (H5); interprocedural facts, where they exist, are per-function
summaries, and two projects keep them in discardable attributes (G1,
G2). §5.6.3 turns this into one solver layer: a maintained program
graph and three solve shapes.

#### 5.6.1 Findings

**A. CIRCT: whole-design analyses are hand-written sparse solvers, not
`DataFlowSolver`.**

A1. *(read)* `circt:lib/Dialect/FIRRTL/Transforms/IMConstProp.cpp`
(1,147 lines) is SCCP over the whole circuit with its own state: one
`DenseMap<FieldRef, LatticeValue>` (`:338`), where `LatticeValue` is a
`PointerIntPair<Attribute, 2, Kind>` (`:170`), eight bytes for
unknown/constant/overdefined; a `SmallPtrSet<Block *>` of executable
blocks (`:341`); one `SmallVector<FieldRef>` worklist of *changed values*
(`:345`); a precomputed `DenseMap<FieldRef, TinyPtrVector<Operation *>>`
of users (`:348`), filled when a block first becomes executable
(`:555-571`); and the interprocedural edge as a map from a module's
output port to the instance results that read it (`:356`). The transfer
function is the op's own `fold` (`:915`). The solve is sequential; the
rewrite runs `mlir::parallelForEach` over modules (`:425-427`); every
map is cleared at the end of the pass (`:430-436`).

A2. *(read)* IMConstProp has a size cut: an op with more than 128
operands is marked overdefined "to prevent regressions" (`:880-886`). It
degrades the answer, never refuses, which is the shape AGENTS.md's
budget rule asks for.

A3. *(read)* `circt:lib/Dialect/FIRRTL/Transforms/IMDeadCodeElim.cpp`
(910 lines) is a backward liveness over the circuit whose state is a
*set of live elements*, `DenseSet<std::variant<Value, FModuleOp,
FInstanceLike, hw::HierPathOp>>` (`:110-122`): a dead element costs
nothing, a live one a hash entry. One worklist of that variant
(`:121`), dispatched once by `std::get_if` (`:493-503`). It reads
upstream's effects to decide what is a root (`hasUnknownSideEffect`,
`:37-41`: `isMemoryEffectFree`, `hasSingleEffect<Allocate>`,
`hasSingleEffect<Read>`). Sequential solve, then module signatures
rewritten sequentially and bodies in `parallelForEach` (`:505-522`).

A4. *(read)* `circt:include/circt/Support/InstanceGraph.h` is the call
graph CIRCT's whole-design passes share, held in the `AnalysisManager`
(`:199-204`) and *kept up to date by the passes that change it*
(`addModule`, `erase`, `replaceInstance`, `:286-305`) instead of being
rebuilt: nodes and edges are intrusive lists (`InstanceRecord` with
`nextUse`/`prevUse`, `:59-100`), the module-to-node map one
`DenseMap<Attribute, InstanceGraphNode *>` (`:321`). Construction walks
modules in `mlir::parallelFor` to collect instances, then links them
sequentially (`circt:lib/Support/InstanceGraph.cpp:66-98`). The node and
edge kinds are op interfaces declared in ODS
(`circt:include/circt/Support/InstanceGraphInterface.td`:
`InstanceOpInterface`, `ModuleOpInterface`), not an `isa` list.

A5. *(read)* The only `DataFlowSolver` uses in CIRCT's `lib/` are the
Comb integer-range passes (`circt:lib/Dialect/Comb/Transforms/
IntRangeOptimizations.cpp:118-131`, `IntRangeAnnotations.cpp:103`),
which load `DeadCodeAnalysis` and `IntegerRangeAnalysis` on an op the
pass manager anchors (a `Pass<"comb-int-range-narrowing">` with no
fixed op, `circt:include/circt/Dialect/Comb/Passes.td:25`): a scoped,
local use. No whole-circuit analysis is on the framework.

A6. *(read)* CIRCT's other whole-design solver is
`circt:lib/Dialect/FIRRTL/Transforms/InferWidths.cpp` (2,303 lines): it
*translates* the circuit into a constraint system of its own and never
solves over the IR. Expressions are hash-consed structs
(`VarExpr`, `KnownExpr`, `AddExpr`, `MaxExpr`, ..., kinds in one
`enum class Kind : uint8_t`, CRTP `ExprBase<Derived, Kind>` supplying
`classof`, `:116-160`), allocated from one `BumpPtrAllocator` by an
`InternedAllocator<T>` that requires `is_trivially_destructible` at
compile time (`:347-369`); the `ConstraintSolver` (`:615-700`) owns the
arena, so the whole solve is freed in one release. The IR is read once
to build the system, the system is solved, and the solution is written
back.

**B. Why CIRCT is built this way: the history.**

B1. *(read, GitHub)* IMConstProp predates `DataFlowSolver`: scaffolded
2021-03-26 (`06adb4f96`, "part of Issue #803/804", the Scala FIRRTL
compiler's dead code elimination) and made interprocedural 2021-04-10
(`84c82d124`), more than a year before upstream's framework landed
(D126751, June 2022). Nothing in its history moves it onto the
framework; every later change tunes its own representation:
- `dca576b21` (2021-04-10): "The general idea of SCCP is that you want
  to drive things to 'overdefined' as rapidly as possible (reduce number
  of lattice state changes). This changes the algorithm to walk the
  use-list for things that change before walking a block."
- `f2b68ae02` (2022-02-08): worklist from queue to stack, "Improves
  locality. See 20% performance improvement on large designs."
- `22ce21c07` (2023-03-20, #4795): the lattice key became `FieldRef`
  (value plus integer field id) rather than an access path, "mostly
  because of compile time performance reasons ... we can refer
  subelements by simple integer additions/subtractions"; measured cost
  9.1 s to 17.5-20.2 s of IMConstProp on an internal design.
- `704a4f696` (2023-05-31, #5291): "in the internal designs there are
  operations with 65536 operands which caused regression in IMCP ...
  giving up such operations, especially when there are more than 128
  operands" (finding A2).

B2. *(read, GitHub)* The parallel rewrite was a plain refactor,
"Parallelize the rewrite phase of IMConstProp. NFC." (`732c38029`,
2021-06-17), made the day the pass moved onto the shared instance graph
("Switch to InstanceGraph. NFC.", `42ff2ccbd`). The solve has stayed
sequential for five years; only the rewrite, which touches one module
at a time, is parallel.

B3. *(read)* Upstream's framework was reviewed without a memory
measurement. In D126751 its author wrote: "The reason I didn't have a
backreference to DataFlowSolver in each AnalysisState is because I
didn't want to increase the size of AnalysisState, but I haven't
checked the memory usage on large programs yet so this point is a little
moot" (reviews.llvm.org/D126751, Mogball, 2022-06-09), and kept the
dependents on each state "to skip a map lookup when propagateIfChanged
is called". The RFC's own numbers are times only (16 s against
"forever", 5 s against 24 s; discourse.llvm.org/t/63340, page 1).

B4. *(read, Discourse)* The framework was designed *against* DFX, for
extensibility, not scale. In the RFC thread (page 2) its author says "I
studied the attributor design (as well as IREE's port of the attributor
to MLIR)" and "analysis state == attribute" (post 29); the Attributor's
abstract attribute is "both the 'analysis state' and 'analysis'
itself", and "I intentionally designed the framework to separate these
two concepts" (post 31); he began by "taking IREE's port of the
Attributor, cleaning it up", found "too many downstream/custom use
cases" that would not extend smoothly, and wrote a new framework,
borrowing "mainly its dependency-management ideas" (post 38). The
stated goal, quoted again in discourse.llvm.org/t/71088 (post 7): "My
main concern was the extensibility of attributes with respect to the
extensibility of MLIR ... I want different dialects to plug into the
same set of attributes." IREE's reply (stellaraccident, post 40): DFX
"works for the IR target it was built for", "Most of the coupling is in
the Explorer class, which could be generalized", and whoever does it
should build "with the grain". No reply discusses memory or the cost of
a state per value. *(conjecture)* So upstream's state model (a heap
state per anchor, uniqued program points, eager visiting) is the price
of open extensibility, which a closed whole-program compiler such as
ours does not need; CIRCT and IREE never had a reason to move.

B5. *(read, GitHub)* CIRCT did once move a whole-design analysis *off*
the framework, and its reason is ours. `e2eb7c66b` (2023-09-16, #6137,
"[Arc] Improve performance of state update legalization"): "on the
`boom-small` benchmark ... the analysis runs for >130s, which accounts
for >95% of the overall arcilator runtime. This PR replaces the MLIR
dataflow framework implementation of the analysis with a custom one. The
analysis requires only very sparse traversal of the operations, which is
a lot easier to implement with a custom graph of nodes. On `boom-small`
the new implementation takes <200ms." Before
(`lib/Dialect/Arc/Transforms/LegalizeStateUpdate.cpp` at
`e2eb7c66b~1`, lines 60-130): an `AccessState : AnalysisState` holding a
`SmallPtrSet<Access, 1>` per program point, visited by a
`DataFlowAnalysis`. After (same file at `e2eb7c66b`, lines 32-260):
- nodes only for *interesting* ops: state reads and writes, calls,
  callables and ops with regions (`isOpInteresting`, `:32-38`); every
  other op has no node at all;
- two node kinds, `OpAccesses` and `BlockAccesses`, each a struct with a
  parent pointer and small sets of `PointerIntPair<Value, 1, AccessType>`
  (`:46-82`), allocated from `SpecificBumpPtrAllocator`s (`:100-101`);
- propagation *up the region tree* (op to block to parent op, stopping
  where the accessed value is defined, `addOpAccess`/`addBlockAccess`,
  `:219-263`), and across calls by a worklist of callables whose
  argument accesses changed (`:175-207`).
That is 650 times faster on the same problem, from a representation
that gives the uninteresting ops no state. It is the move our ffa91113
made for exclusivity (§3), made three years earlier in CIRCT. The pass
was later deleted with
the rewrite of Arc's state lowering (no `LegalizeStateUpdate.cpp` at
`c9141501`).

**C. IREE: DFX, the Attributor ported to MLIR.**

C1. *(read)* `iree:compiler/src/iree/compiler/Dialect/Util/Analysis/DFX/`
(1,700 lines: `Solver`, `Element`, `State`, `DepGraph`) is "Forked from
the LLVM Attributor ... Unlike the Attributor the solver is only for
performing analysis and does no manifestation" (`DFX/Solver.h:21-29`).
Added 2021-09-14 (`c757af7c1`, "a port of the LLVM Attributor to
MLIR/IREE concepts ... something that we could try to upstream"). Why it
is not upstream: "there are some missing core interfaces that keeps it
locked here ...: tied operands (generalized view-like op interface),
globals, and reference types" (`Solver.h:35-39`).

C2. *(read)* The state model, against upstream's (§5.1 A1-A2):
- **Elements are created on demand.** `getElementFor<T>(querying, pos,
  resolution)` looks up `(T::ID, Position)` in one
  `DenseMap<pair<const char *, Position>, AbstractElement *>`
  (`Solver.h:311-312`) and creates the element only when some element
  asks for it (`:114-172`). An analysis seeds only the values of the
  type it is about: `ResourceUsageAnalysis` seeds
  `walkValuesOfType<IREE::Stream::ResourceType>` and nothing else
  (`iree:.../Stream/Analysis/ResourceUsage.cpp:1106-1109`). A value no
  element asks about costs nothing.
- **One arena per solve.** Elements are placement-new'd into a
  `BumpPtrAllocator` the *caller* owns (`Solver.h:48-50`,
  `ResourceUsage.cpp:293`); the solver's destructor runs destructors
  and the arena goes with the analysis object (`Solver.cpp:16-23`).
- **A position is 16 bytes, uniqued by nothing.** `Position` is a
  `PointerIntPair<void *, 2>` (value, returned value, operation, block)
  plus an `unsigned` ordinal (`iree:.../Util/Analysis/Position.h`); no
  `StorageUniquer`, no program point per op.
- **A dependency is one tagged pointer.** `DepGraphNode::deps` is a
  `TinyPtrVector<PointerIntPair<DepGraphNode *, 1>>`, the bit saying
  REQUIRED or OPTIONAL (`DFX/DepGraph.h`); dependencies are recorded
  only during an update and only on elements not yet at a fixpoint
  (`Solver.cpp:201-220`), and an element whose update queried nothing
  is fixed at once (`:183-187`).
- **Invalidity short-circuits.** When an element reaches an invalid
  state, every REQUIRED dependent is driven to its pessimistic fixpoint
  without running its update (`Solver.cpp:56-83`).
- **States are small value types.** `BooleanState`,
  `BitIntegerState<uint16_t, ...>`, `IncIntegerState`,
  `PotentialValuesState<T>` (`DFX/State.h:174-390`) combined with an
  element kind by `StateWrapper<State, ElementKind>` (`:578`), so
  `ValueResourceUsage` carries 13 usage bits in a `uint16_t`
  (`ResourceUsage.cpp:116-138`).

C3. *(read)* DFX has a round budget, and IREE turns it into a refusal.
`Solver::run(int maxIterations = 32)` stops after that many rounds and
drives every element still changing, and every element that depends on
one, to its pessimistic state (`Solver.cpp:136-158`): a sound,
less precise answer. But it then returns `failure()` (`:167`), and the
passes make that a compile error: "failed to solve for usage analysis"
(`iree:.../Stream/Transforms/RefineUsage.cpp:590-594`), "failed to
solve for affinity analysis" (`CloneToConsumers.cpp:194-197`), "failed
to solve for timepoint coverage" (`ElideTimepoints.cpp:1839-1842`).
The affinity solve's budget was raised to 128 as a flag in 2025
(`676115dff`): "it's still risky in the failure cases as it could lead
to _very_ long times to failure. The proper solution is to reduce the
iteration count by somehow dealing with very long tied op chains"
(`Affinity.cpp:30-34`, "Roughly equivalent to operation chain depth").
This is exactly what AGENTS.md forbids: the solver had already produced
a sound answer, and the pass threw it away to refuse the program.

C4. *(read)* DFX's elements reach the IR through IREE's `Explorer`
(`iree:.../Util/Analysis/Explorer.h`, 403 lines; `.cpp` 1,326), a query
layer over a `CallGraph`, an inverse call graph
`DenseMap<Region *, SmallVector<CallOpInterface>>`, a
`SymbolTableCollection`, and per-global load/store lists (`:367-399`),
with traversal *configured by interface, dialect or op*
(`setOpInterfaceAction`, `setDialectAction`, `setOpAction`,
`:113-155`). Its walks (`walkDefiningOps`, `walkTransitiveUses`,
`walkIncomingCalls`, `walkReturnOperands`) follow calls, returns,
branch and region successors, tied operands and globals across the whole
program and answer `COMPLETE` or `INCOMPLETE` (`:65-90`), so an
element's update is "join over every definer and every use"
(`ResourceUsage.cpp:1040-1064`). Two costs show: each walk allocates a
fresh `DenseSet<void *>` and `SetVector<Value>` ("Right now we do ~2-3
map lookups per value traversed", `Explorer.cpp:793-798`), and the
explorer is rebuilt by every pass that needs it ("Expensive to create
... Prefer to create and cache this once per pass", `Explorer.h:96-101`);
it is not an `AnalysisManager` analysis.

C5. *(read)* IREE keeps its whole-program solves small by *scoping what
they see*, not by making the solver cheaper: `ResourceUsageAnalysis`
sets `explorer.setOpAction<IREE::Stream::ExecutableOp>(IGNORE)`
("Ignore the contents of executables (linalg goo, etc)",
`ResourceUsage.cpp:1074-1075`), so the device code, which is most of the
IR, is invisible to the host program's analyses.

C6. *(read)* Precomputed facts are smuggled into the solver as a fake
element. `AffinityAnalysis::PrecomputedQueries` (pinned affinities,
`Affinity.h:104-121`) is computed by "an unfortunate full-module walk"
and injected as a `SidebandElement` registered at the root op, which
every element looks up to reach it (`Affinity.cpp:66-104, 182-207`;
"TODO(benvanik): template this as a helper"). DFX has no typed context
for an analysis's own precomputed tables.

**D. IREE: IPO, the fixed-point pipeline, and upstream's framework in
a function's scope.**

D1. *(read)* IREE's interprocedural optimization is not a solver.
`iree:.../Util/Transforms/IPO.cpp` (721 lines) computes one
`FuncAnalysis` per function (`:49-90`): bit vectors for uniform
arguments, used arguments, used results, uniform results, a duplicate
map and the pass-through map, every field indexed by argument or result
position. It analyzes every function first, "Since we're joining data
from across the whole program we can't perform any mutations during
this analysis", then applies the changes (`:674-709`).

D2. *(read)* Convergence across IPO, canonicalization, CSE and global
folding comes from `FixedPointIteratorPass`
(`iree:.../Util/Transforms/FixedPointIterator.cpp`): it reruns a nested
pipeline until no pass sets the discardable attribute
`iree.fixedpoint.modified` on the module, up to `max-iterations`
(default 10), and then fails: "maximum iteration count exceeded in fixed
point pipeline" (`:107-125`). The Stream pipeline uses it twice
(`iree:.../Stream/Transforms/Passes.cpp:131-146, 400-427`). Both halves
are what AGENTS.md rules out: a fact (did anything change) in a
discardable attribute, and a round count that refuses.

D3. *(read)* Where IREE uses upstream's `DataFlowSolver`, it scopes it to
one function by the pass manager. `OptimizeIntArithmeticPass` is a
`Pass<..., "">` (`Passes.td:183`) added under `FunctionLikeNest`
(`iree:.../GlobalOptimization/Passes.cpp:125-130`,
`Stream/Transforms/Passes.cpp:43-51`), so each function gets its own
solver, on its own thread, freed with the pass. Inside, it reruns the
solve after each greedy rewrite round (`solver.eraseAllStates();
solver.initializeAndRun(op)`), because incremental invalidation "currently
results in occasional crashes. See
https://github.com/iree-org/iree/issues/20636"
(`OptimizeIntArithmetic.cpp:586-599`), with a `RewriterBase::Listener`
that erases the states of erased or replaced ops (`:505-524`).
Thirteen IREE files name `DataFlowSolver`; all are in codegen
(per-dispatch) or solve one function at a time. The one module pass
among them, `AnnotateDispatchAssumptions`, keeps one solver but calls
`initializeAndRun` once per function that contains a dispatch, never on
the module (`iree:.../Stream/Transforms/AnnotateDispatchAssumptions.cpp:30-61`),
so its states accumulate across those functions and are freed only with
the pass.

D4. *(read)* IREE recomputes its whole-program DFX analyses per pass.
`HAL::DeviceAnalysis` (an `Explorer`, a `BumpPtrAllocator` and a
`DFX::Solver` as members, `iree:.../HAL/Analysis/DeviceAnalysis.h:104-107`)
is constructed afresh in 14 files, `Stream::AffinityAnalysis` in 9
(*measured*: `grep -rl` over `compiler/src`), because the solver's
state holds raw pointers into the IR and "Modifying the IR invalidates
the state" (`DFX/Solver.h:41-43`). Each analysis's public face is typed:
`std::optional<DeviceSet> lookupDeviceTargets(Value)`,
`std::optional<ResourceUsageBitfield> tryLookupResourceUsage(Value)`
(`DeviceAnalysis.h:51-65`, `ResourceUsage.h`), so a pass never sees a
lattice or an element.

D5. *(read)* In-place results are declared by interface, not by a list
of ops. `Util_TiedOpInterface`
(`iree:.../Util/IR/UtilInterfaces.td:748-1000`) says which results are
tied to which operands, with default methods over a
`tied_operands` attribute (`getTiedResultOperandIndex`,
`isOperandTied`, `getOperandTiedResults`, ...); the Explorer's walks
follow ties through it, and `hasMutatingTiedUses` tells a mutation from a
scheduling pass-through by trait (`TiedResourcePassthrough`) and
interface (`StreamableOpInterface::isMetadata`,
`AsyncAccessOpInterface`) "rather than hardcoded op names"
(`ResourceUsage.cpp:41-92`). The rest of the same analysis is still a
`TypeSwitch` of 58 cases (*measured*, `.Case` count in
`ResourceUsage.cpp`).

D6. *(read)* IREE's one whole-program answer that later passes rely on
is written into *types*, once. `RefineUsagePass` runs
`ResourceUsageAnalysis` and rewrites every `!stream.resource<*>` to a
lifetime (`External`, `Staging`, `Constant`, `Variable`, `Transient`)
chosen from the usage bits (`convertUsageToLifetime`,
`iree:.../Stream/Transforms/RefineUsage.cpp:40-93`); every later pass
reads the type, not the analysis. Its author's own note on the cost of
the mapping: "TODO(benvanik): explode the concept of lifetime so we can
just specify these bits directly and avoid needing the mapping - doing
this the way we are today is a lossy operation that makes subsequent
analysis more difficult" (`:60-63`). The analysis answer becomes the
representation, which is what our grades in `!idr.q<...>` already are;
the lesson in the TODO is that the type should hold the answer's own
bits, not a lossy summary of them.

**E. Flang (at the pin): function-local analyses, upstream's alias
analysis aggregator, and opt-in caches.**

E1. *(read)* Flang has no whole-program solver. `flang/lib` names no
`CallGraph` and calls no `parallelFor`; its pipeline nests every pass on
the top-level ops (`addNestedPassToAllTopLevelOperations`,
`llvm:flang/lib/Optimizer/Passes/Pipelines.cpp:45,191,248,299`), so the
pass manager supplies the parallelism. Its one `DataFlowSolver` user,
`StackArrays` (`llvm:flang/lib/Optimizer/Transforms/StackArrays.cpp`), is
a `Pass<"stack-arrays", "mlir::func::FuncOp">`
(`llvm:flang/include/flang/Optimizer/Transforms/Passes.td:374`) that
loads `SparseConstantPropagation`, `DeadCodeAnalysis` and its own
`DenseForwardDataFlowAnalysis<LatticePoint>` on one function
(`StackArrays.cpp:113-126, 367-377`).

E2. *(read)* Flang's alias analysis is demand-driven, not a solve:
`fir::AliasAnalysis::getSource(Value)` walks the use-def chain backwards
to a `Source` (an `ENUM_CLASS SourceKind` ordered from `Null` to
`Unknown`, "`alias()` treats `kind >= Indirect` as MayAlias",
`llvm:flang/include/flang/Optimizer/Analysis/AliasAnalysis.h:49-87`),
with an access path of typed steps (`PathStep::Kind`: `Component`,
`PointerDeref`, `AllocDeref`). It plugs into upstream's
`mlir::AliasAnalysis` aggregator (`addAnalysisImplementation`,
`llvm:flang/lib/Optimizer/Transforms/LoopInvariantCodeMotion.cpp:327`),
so upstream LICM reads it.

E3. *(read)* Flang's answer to repeated effect queries on region ops is
an opt-in, pass-scoped cache. `AliasAnalysisRecursiveEffectsCache`
(`AliasAnalysis.h:540-600`) keeps one `Summary` per op with
`HasRecursiveMemoryEffects` (unknown-read and unknown-write flags, read
and write locations, calls), "so mod-ref queries do not re-walk the
regions of ops with HasRecursiveMemoryEffects"; LICM builds it on its
stack and documents why it is sound for that pass: "Both are
frozen-snapshot caches with no automatic invalidation ... LICM only
hoists pure-read ops, never moves writes, erases ops, or RAUWs values"
(`LoopInvariantCodeMotion.cpp:298-327`). The cache exposes hit and miss
counters for tests.

E4. *(read)* Where an op's effects depend on its operand *types*, Flang
writes them by hand. HLFIR declares
`DeclareOpInterfaceMethods<MemoryEffectsOpInterface>` 24 times
(*measured*, `HLFIROps.td`); `hlfir::AssignOp::getEffects` reads memory
only "when RHS is a variable", and reads an unknown resource when it is
a descriptor (`llvm:flang/lib/Optimizer/HLFIR/IR/HLFIROps.cpp:120-150`).
The FIR dialect defines two resources of its own, `DebuggingResource`
and `VolatileMemoryResource` (`llvm:flang/include/flang/Optimizer/
Dialect/FIROps.h:52-59`).

**F. ClangIR: upstream at the pin, and the incubator's lifetime
checker.**

F1. *(read)* Upstream ClangIR at the pin has no dataflow and no lifetime
analysis: no file under `llvm:clang/lib/CIR` or `clang/include/clang/CIR`
names `DataFlowSolver`, and its transforms are lowering and
simplification (`clang/lib/CIR/Dialect/Transforms/`: `FlattenCFG`,
`HoistAllocas`, `LoweringPrepare`, `GotoSolver`, ...). Its effects in
ODS: 64 `Pure`, 14 `MemRead`, 15 `MemWrite`, 2 `MemAlloc`, 3
`RecursiveMemoryEffects`, 1 hand-written `getEffects` over 315 ops

F3. *(read, incubator `llvm/clangir` at `63412d47`)*
`clang/lib/CIR/Dialect/Transforms/LifetimeCheck.cpp` (2,344 lines) is

G2. *(read, `triton-lang/triton` at `11523f38`)* Triton's module

#### 5.6.2 What it means for idris-mlir

All of this is *conjecture* until wave 2 checks it against our code.

1. **A state exists only for what the problem is about.** That is the
   representation every whole-program solver here shares, and the one
   documented move off upstream's framework (B5, 650 times) and our own
   ffa91113 are both instances of it: IMDCE keeps a set of *live*
   elements, so the dead cost nothing (A3); Arc's access graph has no
   node for an op that neither touches state, calls, nor holds a region
   (B5); DFX creates an element only when another asks for it, and
   seeds only values of the analyzed type (C2). For us the rule has a
   sharper form, because our types carry what Idris proved: the grade in
   `!idr.q<quantity, permission, T, lifetime>` already answers, for many
   values, the question counting or exclusivity asks (a `lin` value is
   used once; an `excl` value is exclusive). So a solver's domain is the
   values whose *type leaves its question open*, read off the type, not
   computed. The type system makes the other states unrepresentable in
   the solver, rather than computed and then found trivial. Wave 2
   counts both sets at stage 1 (W-E2).
2. **One call graph, cached and kept up to date** (A4, H3, D4). CIRCT's
   `InstanceGraph` is the model: an `AnalysisManager` analysis, built
   once (instances collected per module in parallel, linked
   sequentially), mutated in place by the passes that change calls, and
   preserved by name (15 CIRCT pass files do). Our pipeline changes calls in
   defunctionalize, contify, optimize (inline, specialize) and symbol
   DCE; §2 lists `CallCycles`, `Recursion`, `Inline`, `LabelSites` and
   `Support/Scc.cpp`, each of which may derive call-graph facts on its
   own. IREE's 14 rebuilds of `DeviceAnalysis` (D4) are the
   rederivation chain §1 asks us to kill, at whole-program scale.
3. **Interprocedural facts are summaries over the SCC DAG, in a typed
   table, never in attributes** (D1, G1, G2, H3). IREE's `FuncAnalysis`
   (bit vectors indexed by argument and result position) and Triton's
   `CallGraph<T>` (a `DenseMap<FunctionOpInterface, T>`) hold a summary
   per function; Enzyme's newer activity analysis solves each function
   alone bottom-up and reads callees' summaries in `visitExternalCall`.
   Both Enzyme and Triton store summaries as discardable attributes,
   which AGENTS.md forbids for a fact a pass relies on; the summary
   belongs in the analysis object. Recursion is ours: FIRRTL forbids it
   (so CIRCT's `InstanceInfo` is one top-down walk, H3), Triton aborts on
   it, Enzyme's sort fails on it. An SCC whose members are iterated
   until no summary changes is essential complexity, and it terminates
   because summaries are finite lattices joined monotonically: by
   construction, with no round count.
4. **Upstream's framework, where it stays, runs per function** (D3, E1,
   G1, G2). Every project that keeps `DataFlowSolver` in production
   scopes it to one function by the pass manager (IREE's
   `FunctionLikeNest`, Flang's `Pass<..., "mlir::func::FuncOp">`), or
   by a solver per function freed after its answer is copied out
   (Triton). This agrees with §5.1's item 2: the seven files of ours on
   the framework (§2) are per-function solves reading item 3's
   summaries.
5. **A budget degrades a site; it never fails a pass** (A2, C3, D2).
   CIRCT's cut at 128 operands marks one op overdefined and goes on;
   DFX's spent budget drives unfinished elements and their dependents to
   their pessimistic fixpoint, which is sound. What AGENTS.md forbids is
   the step IREE adds on top (`RefineUsage`, `CloneToConsumers`,
   `ElideTimepoints` turning `failure()` into a compile error) and
   `FixedPointIteratorPass`'s round count. The solver layer's rule: an
   optimizing analysis may stop at a budget and finalize pessimistically,
   reporting success; an analysis a refusal rests on (knots, the
   in-place promise) has no budget, and terminates by its lattice.
6. **The honest solver is extract, solve, write back** (A6, B5).
   InferWidths reads the circuit once into a hash-consed constraint
   system in one arena, solves it, and writes widths back; Arc builds a
   node graph of the interesting ops and propagates on it. For us the
   problems that are not per-value dataflow (the defunctionalization
   label solve, exclusivity's program graph, counting's flow graph after
   b8301ebe, `Tail/Frames`' plan) fit this form. MLIR then appears only
   in the extractor and the writer, which run per function in parallel
   (as CIRCT's `InstanceGraph` build and Dedup's hashing do, H5); the
   solve works on plain arrays, testable without MLIR, its memory a sum
   one can write down.
7. **No project threads a fixpoint** (H5). Parallelism in all of them is
   per function or module, before and after the solve. A wavefront over
   the SCC DAG (every SCC whose callees are done, in parallel, as §5.1's
   item 7 suggests) has no precedent in these trees; it is ours to
   design and to measure, and the solve's sequential form is the
   fallback that is never wrong.
8. **Structured walks are linear only without copying state at
   branches** (F3). After defunctionalization our IR is region-structured
   (W-E8), so a forward problem can be one walk of the region tree, as
   ClangIR's `LifetimeCheck` is. But `LifetimeCheck` copies its whole map
   per branch and joins after: at a match of 30,000 cases that is
   b8301ebe's wall ("a candidate bit vector per value, copied along every
   edge"). The representation that removes it: a case's state is a delta
   over the state at the match's entry (an undo log, or a scoped table as
   upstream CSE's `ScopedHashTable` is), and the join folds the deltas.
9. **Queries on a big region op are answered once per op** (C4, E3,
   §5.1 C). Flang memoizes an effect summary per op with
   `HasRecursiveMemoryEffects`, because otherwise every mod-ref query
   re-walks the regions; IREE's Explorer enumerates region successors
   per walk. On a match of tens of thousands of cases a per-query walk
   multiplies by the case count; e96bb991 is this. The solver layer owns
   an op's edge and effect table, built once.
10. **Ladders, once and checked** (H1, H2, H4). Every project keeps some
   ladder, but in one place per dialect (CIRCT's visitors), with the open
   part behind interfaces (IREE's `TiedOpInterface`, CIRCT's
   `InstanceOpInterface`). For our closed dialect the solver's transfer
   functions can be made exhaustive at compile time: the op list
   generated from ODS, a transfer per op required by a concept, a
   missing case a `static_assert` rather than CIRCT's
   `visitInvalidComb` abort (a §5.5 question). The solver's own data are
   value types templated on their lattice (DFX's
   `IntegerStateBase<BaseTy, Best, Worst>`, CIRCT's
   trivially-destructible arena check).

#### 5.6.3 Candidate designs, ranked

Each is *conjecture*; the costs are arithmetic on §3's counts (70,000
functions; V values, which §5.1 H1 puts near 10 million at some point
of the pipeline) and on the structures read above, not measurements.

**Design 1 (ranked first): one solver layer, one program graph, three
solve shapes.** A directory of its own (say `lib/Solver/`), whose
header names, per solver, what upstream lacks and the measurement that
shows it (§4). It holds:

- **`ProgramGraph`**, an `AnalysisManager` analysis on `idr.program`,
  in CIRCT's `InstanceGraph` mould (A4) but over `CallOpInterface` and
  `CallableOpInterface`, and dense: functions numbered once, edges in
  compressed arrays, SCCs and their bottom-up order part of the graph.
  Built by a parallel walk per function and a sequential link; kept up
  to date by the passes that change calls (defunctionalize, contify,
  inline, specialize, symbol DCE) and preserved by name, so no pass
  rebuilds it (deletes: every call-graph or SCC derivation of ours that
  wave 2 finds, W-E1).

  ```cpp
  // A sketch of the shape, not of the names.
  struct FunctionId { std::uint32_t index; };   // dense, stable for the graph

  class ProgramGraph {                           // getAnalysis<ProgramGraph>()
  public:
    explicit ProgramGraph(Operation *program);   // parallel collect, serial link
    std::span<const FunctionId> callees(FunctionId) const;
    std::span<const FunctionId> callers(FunctionId) const;
    std::span<const Scc> bottomUp() const;       // SCCs, callees first
    FunctionOpInterface op(FunctionId) const;
    FunctionId id(FunctionOpInterface) const;    // one table, op to id
    void replaceCall(CallOpInterface from, CallOpInterface to); // in place
  private:
    std::vector<FunctionOpInterface> functions;
    std::vector<std::uint32_t> calleeStart, calleeIds;          // CSR
    std::vector<std::uint32_t> callerStart, callerIds;
    std::vector<Scc> sccs;
  };
  ```

- **Shape S, summaries over the SCC DAG** (item 3): one typed summary per
  function in a `std::vector<S>` indexed by `FunctionId`, solved
  callees-first; inside an SCC, members are re-run until no summary
  grows. The concept states what makes it terminate:

  ```cpp
  template <class S>
  concept Summary = std::regular<S> && requires(S &s, const S &t) {
    { s.join(t) } -> std::same_as<bool>;  // true if s grew; finite height
  };

  template <Summary S, class Transfer>
    requires std::is_invocable_r_v<S, Transfer &, FunctionOpInterface,
                                   SummaryTable<S> const &>
  SummaryTable<S> solveBottomUp(const ProgramGraph &, Transfer &&);
  ```

  Sequential first; a wavefront over SCCs whose callees are done is an
  optimization to measure (item 7). Cost: 70,000 summaries of tens of
  bytes is a few MB; work is each function's transfer times its SCC's
  rounds, bounded by the lattice height, not by a count.
- **Shape L, local solves per function** (item 4): upstream's
  `DataFlowSolver` in a function-anchored pass (patched per §5.1 P1), or
  a structured walk with per-case deltas (item 8), reading shape S's
  table in `visitExternalCall`. Peak memory: the largest function's
  states times the threads, instead of the module's.
- **Shape G, extract, solve, write back** (item 6): an extractor run per
  function in parallel turns the IR into plain arrays (nodes numbered in
  walk order, edges in CSR), a solver over those arrays alone, and a
  writer that walks the functions *in the same order* to put the answer
  back, so neither side needs a map from `Value` to node (an index
  instead of a name; conjecture, valid because the IR does not change
  between extract and write). The answer goes into the IR as a type or
  an op where it is a fact later passes rely on (D6: IREE writes
  resource lifetimes into `!stream.resource`; our grades are already
  this).

What it buys: whole-program facts at a cost proportional to functions
and to the open part of each problem; one place where threading is
decided (extract and write in parallel, solve sequential or by
wavefront); solvers testable on arrays. What it deletes: module-wide
`DataFlowSolver` solves, per-pass rebuilds of call-graph facts, any fact
carried in a discardable attribute (W-E4), and any round count (W-E5).
What it costs: every pass that changes calls keeps `ProgramGraph`
correct (CIRCT's discipline, A4: "These methods are not thread safe ...
only safe when the pass is on a CircuitOp or a ModuleOp"), and summaries
are one per function, not per call site: where a fact needs contexts,
specialization has already made them functions.

**Design 2 (second, inside Design 1): lazy, type-filtered state.**
DFX's two ideas without DFX (C2): a solver keeps state only for values
whose type leaves its question open (item 1), and only once a query
reaches them, in an arena freed with the function's solve. The filter
is the one place the solver could go unsound (a value wrongly left out),
so it is not a C++ predicate but the type's own answer: a type interface
method in ODS (§2's `ValueFacts` is the candidate), one fact in one
place. With dense numbering from shape G, states sit in vectors; §5.1
A4 measures one bit in a `BitVector` at 0.13 B a value, against 2.9 KB
at the pin. Deletes: states for linear and exclusive values in counting
and exclusivity, if wave 2 finds them (W-E2).

**Design 3 (third): upstream's framework, per function, patched.** For
the per-value sparse problems already on it (§2: `Constants`,
`Ranges`, `Borrows`, `Narrow`, `Tail/Returned`, `Effects/Constants`),
keep upstream as it is, scoped to a function as IREE, Flang and Triton
do (D3, E1, G2), with §5.1's container patch. This is the order of
preference of §4 for these; it is ranked third here only because
Designs 1 and 2 decide what is left for it.

**Design 4 (not recommended): port IREE's DFX.** About 3,400 lines with
the Explorer, under Apache-2.0 WITH LLVM-exception. It would bring lazy
states, REQUIRED/OPTIONAL pruning and P2's flat layout at once (C2). It
also brings a vtable per element and per state, state invalidated by any
IR change (rebuilt in 14 IREE passes, D4), a round count whose failure
IREE turns into errors (C3), an Explorer rebuilt per pass (C4), and a
sideband hack for an analysis's own tables (C6). It would be our solver
anyway, without having been designed for our problem; take its ideas
(Design 2), not its code.

**Rejected.** A fixed-point pipeline (IREE's `FixedPointIteratorPass`,
D2): a round count that refuses, and "did anything change" in a
discardable attribute. A module-wide interprocedural `DataFlowSolver`
for any per-value problem (Enzyme's older form, G1; torch-mlir's
`InlineGlobalSlots`, G3): the state model of §3, and the one CIRCT left
(B5).

#### 5.6.5 Notes for other lanes

- **§5.1 mlir-analysis.** DFX already has your P2 layout: one flat
  `DenseMap<pair<const char *, Position>, AbstractElement *>` and
  elements bump-allocated from a caller's arena (C2), and keeps
  dependents in a `TinyPtrVector<PointerIntPair<..., 1>>`, which avoids
  the 64-bucket minimum; *(conjecture from the structures)* about 60 B
  an element plus a 32-byte map bucket. The framework's author designed
  it against DFX, for extensibility (B4). IREE could not make the
  framework's incremental invalidation work (iree-org/iree#20636, D3).
  CIRCT's Arc measured 130 s against 200 ms for the same analysis on
  and off the framework (B5).
- **§5.2 mlir-ods.** ClangIR generates its LLVM lowering patterns from
  fields on its base op class with a backend of its own
  (`CIRLoweringEmitter.cpp`, F2): a model for `idris-mlir-tblgen`, and
  for generating our lowering. HLFIR writes 24 `getEffects` by hand
  because an op's effects depend on its operands' types (E4): a case ODS
  cannot declare, which the redesign should make unrepresentable rather
  than hand-write. IREE's `TiedOpInterface` (D5) declares in-place
  results with default methods over one attribute. Flang defines its own
  resources (`DebuggingResource`, `VolatileMemoryResource`, E4).
- **§5.3 llvm.** DFX is the Attributor minus manifestation, plus an
  Explorer (C1, C4); CIRCT's `InternedAllocator<T>` (A6) is the
  hash-consing-in-an-arena idiom with a compile-time triviality check.
- **§5.5 cxx26.** CIRCT's CRTP visitors with a `HANDLE` macro, Triton's
  walk orders as template parameters dispatched with `if constexpr`,
  DFX's states templated on non-type parameters (H1, H4): each a
  candidate for concepts and for an exhaustive visitor over a generated
  op list (item 10).
- **§5.7 library.** IMConstProp's tuning history (B1) is SCCP practice
  in numbers: drive to overdefined early, a stack worklist for locality
  (20 percent), integer field ids instead of access paths as keys.
- **§5.8 dialect-design.** IREE writes its analysis answer into the
  resource type and regrets the lossy mapping (D6): put the answer's own
  bits in the type. Flang's `SourceKind` is an ordered enum whose order
  carries meaning (`kind >= Indirect` is MayAlias, E2): a coordinate
  that removes a branch. HLFIR is the "one op, effects by operand type"
  design (E4) that §1's "two ops secretly the same with different
  effects" asks about. FIRRTL forbids recursive instantiation, which is
  why CIRCT's whole-design analyses are single walks (H3); our programs
  recurse.
- **§5.9 mlton.** CIRCT's `FieldRef` (value plus integer field id, B1)
  and Arc's per-op nodes (B5) are MLIR's nearest thing to MLton's
  property lists; Enzyme's bottom-up summaries (G1) and IREE's
  `FuncAnalysis` (D1) to its per-function passes.
- **§5.10 rc.** ClangIR's lifetime checker (F3) models ownership with
  points-to sets and checks loops by two unrolled iterations; its
  `LiveObjectAnalysis::isLastUse` is a last-use analysis for moves,
  close to where Perceus places drops.
- **§5.11 oom.** No project here bounds a solver's memory; CIRCT clears
  every map at the end of the pass (A1), DFX frees its arena with the
  analysis (C2), and IREE warns that a spent affinity budget "could lead
  to _very_ long times to failure" (C3).
