# 5.5 cxx26: C++26 as the pinned clang and libc++ implement it

- Each C++20, 23 and 26 feature that replaces a ladder, a callback, a
  hand-written list or a runtime dispatch: concepts and `requires`,
  `consteval`, deducing `this`, pack indexing, expansion statements
  (`template for`), static reflection (P2996) and annotations, contracts
  (P2900), `std::execution` (P2300), `std::function_ref`,
  `std::copyable_function`, `std::inplace_vector`, `std::expected`,
  `std::flat_map`, `std::generator`, the ranges additions,
  `= delete("reason")`, the `_` placeholder, structured-binding packs,
  constexpr containers, `#embed`, and the rest that bears on this.
- For each: the paper, the draft's section, its status in the pinned
  clang (`clang/www/cxx_status.html` at 7208ba24) and libc++
  (`libcxx/docs/Status/`), the flags it needs, and whether it works with
  LLVM's build (no RTTI, no exceptions).
- Patterns, with sketches: dispatch over a closed set of our own types
  (variant and visit, concept-constrained templates, reflection-generated
  tables); callbacks as templates; enums and tables checked at compile
  time; static polymorphism (CRTP against deducing `this`).
- Compile-time cost of template-heavy code at our size, and C++20
  modules (`import std`) with CMake, clang and MLIR's headers.
- The draft is not in `sources/`: cite eel.is/c++draft or
  github.com/cplusplus/draft and open-std.org papers, and list what is
  worth snapshotting.

#### 5.5.1 Findings

**A. The toolchain at the pin**

1. **The pinned clang has none of C++26's three large language
   features usable.** P2996 reflection is "No" (*read*:
   `clang/www/cxx_status.html`, the C++2c table). What exists is the
   start of the upstreaming: a cc1-only `-freflection`, refused below
   C++26 (*read*: `clang/lib/Frontend/CompilerInvocation.cpp:604`,
   `clang/test/Driver/reflection-requires-cxx26.cpp`), and a `^^`
   operator that accepts builtin types and nothing else (*read*:
   `clang/lib/Parse/ParseReflect.cpp:19-50`, "Only supports builtin types
   for now"); no splice, no `std::meta` functions, no `<meta>` header in
   libc++. Contracts (P2900R14) are "No", with no keyword and no Sema.
   Expansion statements (P1306R5) are "Clang 23 (Partial)": the
   enumerating form (`template for (auto x : {a, b})`) and the
   destructuring form (over a tuple-like or aggregate value) work under
   `-std=c++26`, with `break` and `continue`; the iterating form (over a
   constexpr range) is an error, "iterating expansion statements are not
   yet supported" (*read*: `clang/include/clang/Basic/DiagnosticSemaKinds.td:224`,
   `clang/lib/Parse/ParseStmt.cpp:263-278`, `clang/lib/Sema/SemaExpand.cpp`,
   `clang/test/CodeGenCXX/cxx2c-expansion-stmts-control-flow.cpp`,
   `clang/test/CodeGenCXX/cxx2c-iterating-expansion-stmt.cpp`, `XFAIL`).
2. **The C++20, 23 and 26 language features that remove ladders are
   all there** (*read*: `clang/www/cxx_status.html`;
   `clang/lib/Frontend/InitPreprocessor.cpp:653-767` for the macros):
   concepts (Clang 10, `__cpp_concepts` 202002), `consteval` and its
   propagation (Clang 17), `if consteval` (14), class types as non-type
   template parameters (12), deducing `this` (P0847R7, 18), pack indexing
   (P2662R3, 19; for template names P3670R4, 24, without mangling),
   `= delete("reason")` (P2573R2, 19), the `_` placeholder (P2169R4, 18),
   structured-binding packs (P1061R10, 21), variadic friends (P2893R3,
   20), `static operator()` (16). Absent besides the three above:
   concept and variable-template template parameters (P2841R7), trivial
   unions (P3074R7), constexpr structured bindings (P2686R5), constexpr
   exceptions (P3068R6), erroneous behaviour (P2795R5). Structured-binding
   packs are accepted only in a templated context: `auto [...xs] = p;` in
   a plain function is "pack declaration outside of template" (*read*:
   `clang/lib/Sema/SemaDeclCXX.cpp:921-923`; *measured (host)*, the same
   diagnostic).
3. **`#embed` works in C++ as a Clang extension** with an
   extension warning, though the C++ paper (P1967R14) is "No" in the
   status page (*read*: `clang/lib/Lex/PPDirectives.cpp:4074-4081`,
   `clang/include/clang/Basic/DiagnosticLexKinds.td:488`).
4. **Trivial relocation is not in C++26.** P2786R13 was adopted at
   Hagenberg and reverted by P3920R0 at Kona 2025 (*read*:
   `libcxx/docs/Status/Cxx26Papers.csv`, "Reverted by P3920R0"). The
   pinned clang still defines `__cpp_trivial_relocatability` 202502L and
   keeps `__builtin_is_cpp_trivially_relocatable` and
   `__builtin_trivially_relocate` (*read*:
   `clang/lib/Frontend/InitPreprocessor.cpp:767`,
   `clang/include/clang/Basic/BuiltinTraits.td:458-465`,
   `clang/include/clang/Basic/Builtins.td:3327`). Nothing to plan on.
5. **The pinned libc++** (*read*: `libcxx/docs/Status/Cxx{20,23,26,29}Papers.csv`,
   `libcxx/include/version`). Complete: `std::expected` with monadic
   operations (202606L), `flat_map` and `flat_set`, member `visit`,
   `optional<T&>`, `function_ref` (LLVM 24, with P3961R1's single
   indirection), `constant_wrapper`, type ordering (P2830R10), and the
   ranges `to`, `zip`, `enumerate`, `chunk_by`, `concat`, `fold`,
   `stride`, `repeat`, `join_with`, `indices`. Missing:
   `move_only_function` (its macro is commented out,
   `libcxx/include/version:536`), `copyable_function`, `inplace_vector`
   (`version:617`, commented out), `std::generator`, `std::execution`
   (P2300R10 and everything after it), `std::hive`, `indirect` and
   `polymorphic`, the standard's library hardening (P3471R4), `std::simd`,
   `views::cartesian_product`, `chunk`, `slide`, `cache_latest`. libc++'s
   own hardening modes (none, fast, extensive, debug; no ABI change; set
   per TU before any header, or as the vendor default
   `LIBCXX_HARDENING_MODE`) are not the standard's but exist (*read*:
   `libcxx/docs/Hardening.md`).
6. **LLVM's build is no-RTTI, no-exceptions by default and asks C++17 of
   its clients** (*read*: `llvm/CMakeLists.txt:93`,
   `llvm/cmake/modules/HandleLLVMOptions.cmake:1241-1244`,
   `llvm/cmake/modules/AddLLVM.cmake:52-79`, `-fno-rtti`,
   `-fno-exceptions`, `-funwind-tables`). Every language feature in
   finding 2, and member `visit`, `expected` and concepts, compile and run
   under `-fno-rtti -fno-exceptions` (*measured (host)*, one probe of
   all of them). The library's throwing paths (`expected::value()`,
   `visit` on a valueless variant) become aborts.
7. **C++26 is done, and the large features are coming to clang, not
   here yet.** WG21 finished C++26 at Croydon on 2026-03-28 with
   reflection, contracts, expansion statements and `std::execution`; GCC
   trunk has reflection and contracts (*read*: Sutter's trip report). The
   working draft after Croydon is N5046 (2026-05-12, revises N5032)
   (*read*: open-std.org). Clang's reflection is being upstreamed from
   Bloomberg's fork behind a cc1 flag, the reflect expression first, the
   generative parts later (*read*: the Clang reflection meeting thread
   and clang-p2996 issue 189). *Conjecture*: a usable P2996
   (splices, `members_of`, `define_aggregate`) in a pin we would take is
   more than a year away; P0 cannot rest on it.

**C. How MLIR dispatches, which is where concepts meet interfaces**

8. **MLIR has three dispatch costs, and only one is compile time.** An op
   interface is found by a binary search of the op's sorted
   `(TypeID, Concept*)` vector, then called through the concept's
   function-pointer table (*read*:
   `mlir/include/mlir/Support/InterfaceSupport.h:206-207, 272-281`). A
   runtime `op->hasTrait<T>()` is a virtual call into the op's
   `OperationName::Impl` model, which calls a fold of `TypeID` compares
   over every trait of the op (*read*:
   `mlir/include/mlir/IR/OperationSupport.h:331-334, 611`;
   `mlir/include/mlir/IR/OpDefinition.h:1569-1571, 2016-2019`). On a
   concrete op class, `OpT::hasTrait<T>()` is `constexpr`
   (`OpDefinition.h:1716-1718`), and ODS's own trait verifiers use it in
   `static_assert`s (`OpDefinition.h:1179-1306`). So a C++ concept can
   read an ODS trait at compile time, from the one declaration, without
   restating it.
9. **MLIR erases callbacks on purpose at the walk.** `walk` takes a
   template callback and converts it at once to `function_ref`; the typed
   overload wraps the callback in a lambda that does `dyn_cast<ArgT>` and
   erases that (*read*: `mlir/include/mlir/IR/Visitors.h:275-347`). The
   upstream trade is one out-of-line walk for every callback type, not
   one instantiation per call site. A template walk of ours buys the
   callback's inlining into the loop and pays one instantiation per site
   (D, finding 13).
10. **MLIR's ranges are only partly `std::ranges`** (*measured (host)*,
    `static_assert`s against the pinned headers): `ValueRange`,
    `OperandRange`, `ResultRange` and `Block&` are forward ranges but not
    random-access; `Operation::getUsers()` is not a `std::ranges::range`
    at all, nor are the results of `llvm::enumerate` and `llvm::zip`.
    `std::views::enumerate(valueRange)`, `std::views::zip(block,
    valueRange)` and `| std::ranges::to<llvm::SmallVector<Type>>()`
    compile. Mixing the two families works only where the MLIR range
    happens to model the concept.

**D. Compile time, measured (host)**

11. **One TU of MLIR's core headers costs 1.5 s; a precompiled header
    makes it 0.43 s.** `mlir/IR/pch.h` (Builders, BuiltinAttributes,
    BuiltinOps, BuiltinTypes, Dialect, Operation, PatternMatch,
    SymbolTable) preprocesses to 141,000 lines and compiles to an empty
    object in 1.54 s wall at `-O0`; built once as a PCH (1.5 s, 43 MB),
    the same TU takes 0.43 s (*measured (host)*). Upstream already uses
    this: `add_llvm_library` and `add_mlir_library` take
    `PRECOMPILE_HEADERS`, MLIRIR precompiles `mlir/IR/pch.h`, and a
    dependent target reuses the highest-priority PCH of its dependencies
    (*read*: `mlir/include/mlir/IR/pch.h`, `mlir/lib/IR/CMakeLists.txt:53`,
    `llvm/cmake/modules/AddLLVM.cmake:82-147, 586-614`). PCH is turned off
    by default under `LLVM_ENABLE_MODULES`, with GCC, and with clang
    before 18 (*read*: `llvm/cmake/modules/HandleLLVMOptions.cmake:1376-1412`).
12. **`std::visit` falls off a cliff at twelve alternatives.** The pinned
    libc++ dispatches a single-variant `visit` with a `switch` when the
    variant has at most 11 alternatives, and through a constexpr matrix of
    function pointers otherwise (*read*: `libcxx/include/variant:1596-1640`,
    `_LIBCPP_VARIANT_DISPATCH_COUNT 11`, and `:500-590`). Three hundred
    visit sites with an eight-lambda overload set (*measured (host)*,
    `-O2`):

    | dispatch over a closed set of K types, 300 sites | user s | object |
    | --- | ---: | ---: |
    | headers only (baseline) | 0.17 | 0.3 KB |
    | `dyn_cast` ladder (LLVM RTTI), K = 8 / 11 / 16 | 0.62 / 0.70 / 0.93 | 54 / 59 / 69 KB |
    | `std::visit`, K = 8 / 11 | 1.55 / 2.10 | 57 / 65 KB |
    | `std::visit`, K = 16 | 11.3 | 3,042 KB |
    | a ten-line fold `match` (§5.5.3 D2), K = 16 | 2.02 | 68 KB |
    | at `-O0`: `std::visit` K = 16 / fold `match` K = 16 / ladder K = 16 | 6.25 / 1.23 / 0.41 | 15,372 / 1,736 / 372 KB |

    The fold `match` compiles to one bounds check and a jump table at
    `-O2` (*measured (host)*, its assembly). The host's own libc++ (22)
    has no switch path: K = 8 there costs 5.6 s and 1.15 MB, so the
    pinned libc++ is what makes `visit` viable at all.
13. **A template callback costs two to three times a `function_ref` to
    compile.** Three hundred call sites of a recursive tree walk: template
    walk with a `std::invocable` constraint 0.95 s and 100 KB,
    `llvm::function_ref` walk 0.48 s and 75 KB (*measured (host)*, `-O2`,
    against a 0.17 s baseline of the same headers). Per site that is about
    2.6 ms against 1.0 ms. At our size (§2: 84 `function_ref<` and 192
    variant accessors), all of it is seconds; the 1.5 s of headers per TU,
    times every TU, is minutes.

**E. Modules and `import std`**

14. **Clang has C++20 modules, but nothing around it is ready for MLIR.**
    Clang defines `__cpp_modules` as `1`, not 201907L, on purpose
    (*read*: `clang/lib/Frontend/InitPreprocessor.cpp:740-743`, LLVM issue
    71364). libc++'s `std` module is "experimental", and "including
    headers after importing the `std` module may fail" (*read*:
    `libcxx/docs/Modules.md`, LLVM PR61465); every MLIR header includes
    libc++ headers, so a TU would have to include all of MLIR before
    `import std;`, which leaves `import std` nothing to save. The pinned
    CMake 4.2.8 scans modules with Ninja 1.11+ (we have 1.13.2), but
    `import std` sits behind the experimental gate
    `CMAKE_EXPERIMENTAL_CXX_IMPORT_STD` and needs `libc++.modules.json`
    from the compiler's resource directory (*read*:
    `.toolchain/cmake/share/cmake-4.2/Help/manual/cmake-cxxmodules.7.rst:89-107`,
    `Help/prop_tgt/CXX_MODULE_STD.rst`,
    `Modules/Compiler/Clang-CXX-CXXImportStd.cmake`). MLIR ships no module
    map, and LLVM's `LLVM_ENABLE_MODULES` means Clang header modules
    (`-fmodules`), not C++20 named modules, and turns PCH off (*read*:
    `llvm/cmake/modules/HandleLLVMOptions.cmake:743-759, 1377-1380`).
    MLIR's generated code is macro-driven (`GET_OP_CLASSES`,
    `GET_OP_LIST`, `GET_TYPEDEF_CLASSES`), and macros do not cross an
    `import` (*recalled*, [module.import]).
15. **LLVM chose precompiled headers over modules, with numbers.** The
    RFC that brought PCH into the tree measured front-end time down about
    3x and wall time from 139 s to 86 s on a 48-core machine, and says C++
    module builds "don't really help, are fragile, and kill parallelism",
    that C++20 modules "would require substantial refactoring", and that
    Clang header modules reduce parallelism and are fragile (*read*:
    Engelke's RFC on discourse.llvm.org, 2025-12-31). A later RFC on
    `UNITY_BUILD` reports unity builds slightly ahead of PCH (*read*: its
    title and summary only).

**F. Where the C++ meets ODS** (bears on §8 Q1 and on §5.2)

16. **An interface's model is MLIR's monomorphization point.** ODS emits
    one `Model<ConcreteOp>` per op and interface, in which `ConcreteOp` is
    the op's class and `$_op` is a typed instance; a method body that uses
    only `$_raw_op` is placed in one shared callback for all ops, which
    saves the per-op copies (*read*: `mlir/docs/Interfaces.md:556-583`).
    So the runtime dispatch (interface lookup) hands over to code that is
    typed on the concrete op, and that code can call a constrained
    template.
17. **Interface membership and traits are visible to concepts.**
    `OpT::template hasTrait<FooInterface::Trait>()` is a constant
    expression, so a concept can say "an op class implementing
    `SymbolOpInterface`" (*measured (host)*: `static_assert`s on
    `ModuleOp` and `UnrealizedConversionCastOp` against the pinned
    headers).
18. **Upstream indexes interface- and trait-rooted patterns per op.** At
    freeze time, a pattern rooted on an interface or a trait is copied
    into the pattern list of every registered op that has it; only a
    pattern with no root goes to the any-op list that every op tries
    (*read*: `mlir/lib/Rewrite/FrozenRewritePatternSet.cpp:124-141`,
    `mlir/lib/Rewrite/PatternApplicator.cpp:77-83, 154-170`). A list of
    per-op patterns generated in C++ buys no better indexing than
    `OpInterfaceRewritePattern` or `OpTraitRewritePattern`.
19. **A fold expression counts against the bracket depth.** Instantiating
    a fold over N elements is an error past `-fbracket-depth` (default
    256) (*read*: `clang/test/SemaCXX/fold_expr_expansion_limit.cpp`);
    MLIR's `addOperations` uses an `initializer_list` expansion for that
    reason (*read*: `mlir/include/mlir/IR/Dialect.h:277-284`). Our dialect's
    191 ops fit; a fold over a larger generated list does not.

#### 5.5.2 What it means for idris-mlir

All *conjecture* until wave 2 reads the code.

- **"C++26" in our build means the C++20 and 23 core plus a few C++26
  conveniences.** The ladders must be culled with concepts, closed sums,
  pack indexing, the two working forms of `template for`, binding packs
  and deducing `this`. Reflection is not available, so every list it would
  derive comes from TableGen when ODS describes the thing (it already
  holds the records, and `idris-mlir-tblgen` already reads them), or from
  one constexpr table checked by `consteval` when the thing exists only in
  our C++.
- **§2's 48 multi-type `isa<A, B, ...>` and most of its 452 `dyn_cast`s
  are probably over ops**, where the cure is an interface or a trait
  (§5.2's mechanisms), not C++. The ones over our own C++ types (lattice
  values, plan entries, graph nodes, solver states: wave 2 sorts them)
  become closed sums matched exhaustively.
- **The 192 `std::visit`, `get_if` and `holds_alternative` uses** split
  into exhaustive matches (keep, as `match`) and questions whose answer is
  thrown away (`holds_alternative` then `get`): validators, which a match
  that binds the alternative replaces. Any of the 60 variants with more
  than 11 alternatives compiles through the function-pointer matrix today
  (finding 12).
- **The 84 `function_ref<`** split into callbacks threaded through code
  that knows its static type (a template with a concept) and callbacks at
  an MLIR API that takes `function_ref` (`walk`, `replaceUsesWithIf`,
  pattern hooks), which stay as upstream takes them.
- **The 139 `template <` against 20 `requires`/`concept`** says our
  templates are mostly unconstrained: every template signature gets its
  concept.
- **The 10 `virtual`s** are either a closed set (a sum) or an open one
  (keep, or an MLIR interface if it is over IR).
- **Compile time** is dominated by headers, not by our templates, as long
  as sums dispatch through a switch and each solver template is
  instantiated once (findings 11 to 13). If some 250 TUs include MLIR's
  core (*conjecture*; §2 counts 379 files with headers), headers alone are
  about 6 CPU minutes per clean build at the host's speed, and about 2
  with a PCH; every template pattern in §5.5.3 together adds seconds.
- **Modules are not for P0** (findings 14, 15).

#### 5.5.3 Candidate designs, ranked

Every sketch below type-checks with the host compiler against the pinned
libc++ and MLIR headers under `-std=c++2c -fno-rtti -fno-exceptions`
(*measured (host)*, `/tmp/cxx26-probe/sketch`), except where it says it
needs the pinned clang.

**D1. The boundary rule: dispatch once where the set is open, then
monomorphic code.** (Rank 1; the proposed answer to §8 Q1.)

- *Over ops, types and attributes* the set is open to C++: an
  `Operation *` may be any registered op of any loaded dialect. Dispatch
  is MLIR's: an interface, a trait, or a pattern rooted on an op, an
  interface or a trait (finding 18). Never a C++ concept or an
  `isa<A, B, C>` that lists op classes: that is a second copy of a fact ODS
  holds.
- *Below the dispatch* the code is typed: in an interface's
  `Model<ConcreteOp>` (finding 16), in `OpRewritePattern<OpT>`, in an
  external model. There, templates and concepts are the tool, and a
  concept may *read* ODS (a trait, interface membership: finding 17),
  never *list* ops.
- *Over our own C++ types* the set is closed by construction: sums and
  templates, never `isa`, `virtual` or a kind tag.

```cpp
// The fact lives once, in ODS (an Idr_ConsumesOnly trait); the concept reads it.
template <class OpT>
concept ConsumingOp = OpT::template hasTrait<idr::OpTrait::ConsumesOnly>();

// Typed code below the dispatch, generic over op classes, checked at compile time.
template <ConsumingOp OpT>
LogicalResult lowerConsume(OpT op, PatternRewriter &rewriter);
```

```tablegen
// The dispatch, once, by MLIR's mechanism; ODS instantiates the default
// body per op, so `$_op` is the concrete class and the template above is
// monomorphized per op without a list written anywhere.
def Idr_ConsumeLowering : OpInterface<"ConsumeLowering"> {
  let methods = [InterfaceMethod<"lower the op's consumption",
    "::mlir::LogicalResult", "lowerConsume",
    (ins "::mlir::PatternRewriter &":$rewriter), [{}],
    /*defaultImplementation=*/[{ return ::idr::lowerConsume($_op, rewriter); }]>];
}
```

  What it buys: the 48 multi-type ladders and the trait-restating ladders
  of §2 ("isa<> ladders restating trait classes") have nowhere to live;
  the per-op code is typed (generated accessors, compile-time trait
  checks) without `cast<>`. What it costs: one instantiation per op and
  interface (191 ops; a body that needs no concrete type goes in a
  `$_raw_op` shared callback). What it deletes: every `isa` ladder over op
  classes, every hand list of ops in C++.

**D2. Closed sums of our own types: `std::variant` plus one `match`.**
(Rank 2.)

A sum is a `std::variant` of alternatives that each carry only their
legal data (a lattice value is `Bottom | Exact | Range | Top`, not a
value plus flags). Every use is one exhaustive `match`: an unhandled
alternative is a compile error (no viable call in the overload set), a
check an `isa` ladder never had. `holds_alternative` and `get_if` are
banned outside `match` itself: they ask and throw the answer away.

```cpp
template <class... Fs> struct Cases : Fs... { using Fs::operator()...; };

// One switch on the index, from a fold: a jump table at -O2 for any
// number of alternatives up to the bracket depth (findings 12, 19).
template <class V, class... Fs>
constexpr decltype(auto) match(V &&v, Fs &&...fs) {
  Cases<std::decay_t<Fs>...> cases{std::forward<Fs>(fs)...};
  using R = decltype(cases(std::get<0>(std::forward<V>(v))));
  return [&]<std::size_t... I>(std::index_sequence<I...>) -> R {
    if constexpr (std::is_void_v<R>) {
      (void)((v.index() == I ? (cases(*std::get_if<I>(&v)), true) : false) || ...);
    } else {
      std::optional<R> r;   // always assigned: every alternative is handled
      (void)((v.index() == I ? (r.emplace(cases(*std::get_if<I>(&v))), true) : false) || ...);
      return *std::move(r);
    }
  }(std::make_index_sequence<std::variant_size_v<std::remove_cvref_t<V>>>{});
}

struct Bottom {};
struct Exact { int64_t value; };
struct Range { int64_t lo, hi; };
struct Top {};
using Constant = std::variant<Bottom, Exact, Range, Top>;

constexpr Constant join(const Constant &a, const Constant &b) {
  return match(a,
      [&](Bottom) -> Constant { return b; },
      [&](Top) -> Constant { return Top{}; },
      [&](const Exact &x) -> Constant { /* match(b, ...) */ },
      [&](const Range &r) -> Constant { /* match(b, ...) */ });
}
static_assert(std::holds_alternative<Range>(join(Exact{1}, Exact{3})));  // in a test only
```

  With the pinned clang, the fold can be an enumerating expansion
  statement (`template for (constexpr std::size_t i : {I...}) if
  (v.index() == i) return cases(*std::get_if<i>(&v));` then
  `std::unreachable()`), which drops the `optional` (*conjecture*: not
  compiled; the host clang has no `template for`). What it buys: the
  exhaustiveness check, one dispatch form for the whole tree, a switch at
  any size (K = 16: 2.0 s and 68 KB against `std::visit`'s 11.3 s and
  3 MB for 300 sites). What it costs: 3 to 4 ms of compile per site over
  a `dyn_cast` ladder (finding 12). What it deletes: `std::visit` (or it
  stays only on variants of at most 11 alternatives), every
  `holds_alternative` and `get_if` outside `match`, every kind enum
  beside a class hierarchy of our own, and LLVM-style `classof` on our
  own non-IR classes. The limit (Brooks): two alternatives that are the
  same state with different flags are one alternative, and two states
  that differ in what they carry are two; the sum is designed, not
  transliterated from the old ladder.

**D3. The solver layer's C++ shape: concepts for what a solver needs,
dense typed indices for its state.** (Rank 3; the C++ half of the
"honest solvers" directive. What the solvers compute is §5.1's, §5.3's
and §5.7's.)

A solver is a function template over a graph and a lattice, each named by
a concept, with its state in a vector indexed by a node id that only the
graph mints. The composition is in the types (a product lattice is a
struct of lattices; D4c joins it field by field), and the state model
that §3's walls came from (a lattice per value in a hash map, a state per
program point) cannot be written by accident, because the solver has no
map to put it in.

```cpp
// A typed index: only the graph that owns the nodes mints one, so a lookup
// by it cannot miss (a lookup that can miss is a missing index). The sketch
// leaves Id an aggregate; the real one has a private constructor and names
// its graph (or a pass key) as friend.
template <class Tag> struct Id { std::uint32_t raw; friend bool operator==(Id, Id) = default; };
template <class IdT, class T> class IndexVector {
  std::vector<T> items;
public:
  explicit IndexVector(std::size_t n, const T &init = T{}) : items(n, init) {}
  T &operator[](IdT id) { return items[id.raw]; }
  T &operator[](std::uint32_t) = delete("index by the graph's Id, not a raw integer");
};

template <class L>
concept Lattice = std::regular<L> && requires(L &a, const L &b) {
  { L::bottom() } -> std::same_as<L>;
  { a.join(b) } -> std::same_as<mlir::ChangeResult>;   // upstream's own change type
};
template <class G>
concept FlowGraph = requires(const G &g, typename G::Node n) {
  { g.size() } -> std::convertible_to<std::uint32_t>;
  { g.successors(n) } -> std::ranges::forward_range;
};

template <FlowGraph G, Lattice L, class Transfer>
  requires std::is_invocable_r_v<L, Transfer &, typename G::Node, const L &>
IndexVector<typename G::Node, L> solveForward(const G &g, Transfer transfer,
                                              llvm::ArrayRef<typename G::Node> roots);

// One instantiation per solver, in one TU; every other TU sees only this:
extern template IndexVector<ExclGraph::Node, Excl>
solveForward<ExclGraph, Excl, ExclTransfer>(const ExclGraph &, ExclTransfer,
                                            llvm::ArrayRef<ExclGraph::Node>);
```

  The transfer function is a template parameter, not a `function_ref`:
  the solver's inner loop is where inlining it pays, and the static type
  is known. Threading stays explicit and outside: a solve is a value
  computed from a graph, so independent solves (per function, per SCC)
  run under the pass manager or `parallelFor` without shared state (§4,
  §5.3). What it costs: one instantiation per (graph, lattice, transfer),
  paid once with `extern template`. What it deletes: `DenseMap<Value, L>`
  as solver state, `function_ref` transfer callbacks, and any virtual
  "analysis" base of our own.

**D4. One list, one place: TableGen for what ODS describes, a checked
table for the rest, binding packs for aggregates.** (Rank 4.)

- *(a) A list over IR is generated.* `GET_OP_LIST` is already the
  dialect's op classes as a comma list (what `addOperations<>` consumes,
  finding 19), `idris-mlir-tblgen` already reads every record, and LLVM's
  searchable tables generate keyed lookups from records (§5.3). An op's
  runtime symbol (`Idr_Runtime<"symbol">`), its refusal category, its
  effect row: each is emitted to C++ and to Idris from the one record,
  never typed into a C++ `switch`.
- *(b) A list that exists only in our C++* is one `constexpr` table,
  indexed by its enum and checked at compile time:

```cpp
enum class Effect : uint8_t { Read, Write, Alloc, Free, Count };
struct EffectRow { Effect effect; llvm::StringLiteral name; bool mayAlias; };
inline constexpr std::array effectTable{
    EffectRow{Effect::Read, "read", true},   EffectRow{Effect::Write, "write", true},
    EffectRow{Effect::Alloc, "alloc", false}, EffectRow{Effect::Free, "free", true}};
consteval bool indexedByEnum(const auto &table) {
  for (std::size_t i = 0; i < table.size(); ++i)
    if (std::to_underlying(table[i].effect) != i) return false;
  return table.size() == std::to_underlying(Effect::Count);
}
static_assert(indexedByEnum(effectTable), "one row per Effect, in enum order");
constexpr llvm::StringLiteral nameOf(Effect e) { return effectTable[std::to_underlying(e)].name; }
```

  (The example is a shape, not a proposal: our effects are MLIR's, §1.)
- *(c) A list of an aggregate's fields is the aggregate.* A product of
  facts joins, compares and prints field by field with a structured-binding
  pack, so adding a field cannot miss a case:

```cpp
struct Facts { bool mayCrash; bool doesIO; uint8_t grade; };
template <class P> constexpr bool joinFields(P &into, const P &from) {
  auto &[...dst] = into;          // a pack: templated context only (finding 2)
  const auto &[...src] = from;
  bool changed = false;
  ((changed |= (dst | src) != dst, dst = dst | src), ...);
  return changed;
}
```

- *(d) A list over types* is a `TypeList<Ts...>` read with pack indexing
  (`Ts...[I]`) and walked with a fold or, with the pinned clang, an
  enumerating `template for (auto t : {std::type_identity<Ts>{}...})`
  (*read*: the enumerating form takes heterogeneous braced lists,
  `clang/test/SemaCXX/cxx2c-expansion-stmts.cpp:35`); never a recursive
  metafunction.

  What it deletes: hand-written `switch`es over kinds, parallel arrays
  matched by position, X-macros, string names compared as identities.
  What reflection would add later (D9) is only (b) and (c) without the
  boilerplate; (a) stays TableGen even then, because the Idris side reads
  the same records (§2).

**D5. Deducing `this` for our own static polymorphism; MLIR's CRTP stays
MLIR's.** (Rank 5.)

```cpp
struct JoinOps {   // one non-template base, not JoinOps<Derived>
  template <class Self, class... Xs>
  bool joinAll(this Self &self, const Xs &...xs) { return (self.join(xs) | ... | false); }
};
struct Bits : JoinOps { uint64_t v = 0; bool join(const Bits &o); };

auto depth = [&](this auto self, Node n) -> int {   // a recursive lambda, no function_ref
  int d = 0;
  for (Node s : g.successors(n)) d = std::max(d, 1 + self(s));
  return d;
};
```

  Where a mixin of ours uses CRTP (`Base<Derived>` with `static_cast`),
  deducing `this` replaces it with one base class: fewer instantiations,
  no cast, no way to name the wrong derived class. MLIR's CRTP APIs
  (`Op<ConcreteOp, Traits...>`, `OpInterface`, `PassWrapper`,
  `OpRewritePattern<OpT>`) are the interface to upstream and stay as they
  are. An explicit-object member function cannot be virtual, which is the
  point.

**D6. Compile time: precompiled headers first, then measure.** (Rank 6.)

1. One precompiled header for `foreign/idr` (MLIR's `pch.h` set, LLVM's
   ADT, our dialect's headers), through `PRECOMPILE_HEADERS` if we build
   with `add_mlir_library`, or `target_precompile_headers`; with ccache,
   `-Xclang -fno-pch-timestamp` as LLVM sets it
   (`HandleLLVMOptions.cmake:1458-1466`). Host measurement: header cost
   per TU 1.54 s to 0.43 s (finding 11); upstream's measurement: about 3x
   on the front end (finding 15). An installed LLVM's PCH cannot be
   reused by an out-of-tree project (CMake's `REUSE_FROM` names a target
   of the same build; *recalled*), so the PCH is ours. A PCH fixes the
   macros every TU sees, so a per-TU setting that must precede all
   headers (libc++'s hardening mode, `Hardening.md`) becomes a per-target
   one; the risks LLVM's RFC names are hidden missing includes and name
   collisions (finding 15).
2. A `-ftime-trace` build, summarized per header and per template, run
   when the C++ changes, so a template that costs seconds is seen.
3. `extern template` and one explicit instantiation per solver (D3).
4. Sums through `match` (D2); type-list code through pack indexing and
   folds, never recursion; folds under 256 elements (finding 19).
5. Templates for callbacks in hot loops; `function_ref` at MLIR's APIs
   and in cold hooks (findings 9, 13).
6. Interface bodies that need no concrete op in `$_raw_op` shared
   callbacks (finding 16).

  Rejected for P0: unity builds (they merge anonymous namespaces and
  hide missing includes; *conjecture* on our code), C++20 modules (D7).

**D7. Modules and `import std`: not in P0; revisit on three conditions.**
(Rank 7.) Modules come back when libc++'s `std` module is no longer
experimental and an `#include` after `import std` works (PR61465), when
CMake's `import std` leaves its experimental gate, and when MLIR or LLVM
ships a module, or we decide to wrap MLIR in a global-module-fragment
module of our own (`module; #include "mlir/IR/..."; export module
idr.mlir; export using mlir::Operation; ...`), with the dialect's `.inc`
files inside our own module units because their macros do not cross an
import. Until then PCH does what modules would do for headers, without
the build-graph serialization LLVM's RFC measured.

**D8. Vocabulary at the MLIR boundary.** (Rank 8.)

- *Failure*: `LogicalResult` and `FailureOr<T>` when the diagnostic has
  gone to the context (upstream's idiom); `std::expected<T, Reason>` when
  the failure is data the caller branches on (a refusal's category,
  generated from ODS: AGENTS.md puts every refusal's category there);
  `llvm::Error` and `llvm::Expected` in the driver, as the skill says.
- *Maps*: `IndexVector` (D3) when the keys are dense ids we mint;
  `DenseMap` when keys are pointers or sparse (upstream's idiom, §5.3);
  `flat_map` for small, ordered, read-mostly maps whose iteration order
  must be deterministic.
- *Ranges*: LLVM's adaptors (`enumerate`, `zip_equal`, `map_range`,
  `make_filter_range`) over MLIR's ranges, because not all of them are
  `std::ranges` (finding 10); `std::ranges` and `std::views` over our own
  containers. `llvm::zip_equal` checks equal lengths
  (`llvm/include/llvm/ADT/STLExtras.h:852-860`); `std::views::zip` and
  `llvm::zip` truncate silently, a check thrown away.
- *Hardening*: libc++'s extensive mode in debug and test builds of our
  tools, measured for release; set as the bootstrap's
  `LIBCXX_HARDENING_MODE` since our libc++ is pinned and static, or per TU
  before any header.
- *Contracts*: absent at the pin; a precondition is a type first, an
  `assert` meanwhile.

**D9. When the pin has reflection (not P0).** (Rank 9, recorded so no
one plans for it now.) P2996 with the iterating `template for` would
turn D4b and D4c into `std::meta::enumerators_of(^^Effect)` and
`nonstatic_data_members_of(^^Facts, ctx)`, and `define_aggregate` could
build a product lattice from a list of component types; annotations
(P3394R4) could tag C++ declarations. None of it touches D4a: a fact
about IR stays in ODS, because TableGen is what feeds both C++ and the
Idris side. Reflection is less of a lever for us than it looks.

#### 5.5.5 Notes for other lanes

- **mlir-ods (§5.2):** interface- and trait-rooted patterns are indexed
  per op at freeze (finding 18), so they do not cost what an any-op
  pattern does (§3's 75 million attempts); `$_raw_op` shared callbacks
  (finding 16) cut per-op instantiations; `OpT::hasTrait<>()` is
  `constexpr` (finding 8), so a trait with `verifyTrait` can also be
  checked by `static_assert` in C++ that is typed on the op.
- **llvm (§5.3):** `llvm::function_ref` and `std::function_ref` are the
  same tool; libc++ at the pin has no `move_only_function`, so
  `llvm::unique_function` stays; `TypeSwitch` is a chain of `dyn_cast`s
  (`llvm/include/llvm/ADT/TypeSwitch.h:103, 182`), not a jump table.
- **mlir-analysis (§5.1), library (§5.7):** D3's dense, typed state is
  the C++ form of a scoped, compact solver state; `ChangeResult` is
  upstream's and can be shared.
- **dialect-design (§5.8):** D1 assumes facts live in ODS traits and
  interfaces that C++ can read at compile time on a concrete op class.
- **oom (§5.11):** libc++'s hardening and `-fno-exceptions` turn library
  precondition failures into traps, never exceptions; an allocation
  failure in libc++ under `-fno-exceptions` aborts (*recalled*, to check).
